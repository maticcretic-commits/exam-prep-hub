Biology · Cell & biomolecules

Cell essentials

  • Prokaryotes lack a membrane-bound nucleus and organelles; their 70S ribosomes contain 50S + 30S subunits.
  • Eukaryotic cytoplasmic ribosomes are 80S (60S + 40S); mitochondria and chloroplasts have 70S ribosomes.
  • Plasma membrane follows the fluid mosaic model: a lipid bilayer with mobile proteins. Simple diffusion needs no ATP; active transport moves against a gradient using energy.
  • RER synthesises secretory/membrane proteins; SER synthesises lipids and helps detoxification; Golgi modifies, sorts and packages.
  • Lysosomes contain acid hydrolases; peroxisomes contain oxidative enzymes and catalase.

Biology · Genetics & molecular basis

Mendelian core

  • Monohybrid F₂ phenotype ratio under complete dominance: 3:1; genotype ratio: 1:2:1.
  • Independent assortment applies when genes assort independently; linkage reduces recombinant frequency.
  • A test cross pairs an individual showing the dominant phenotype with a homozygous recessive individual.

DNA → RNA → protein

  • DNA replication is semiconservative and proceeds 5′→3′. Leading strand is continuous; lagging strand forms Okazaki fragments.
  • Transcription uses the DNA template strand to make RNA. RNA polymerase reads template 3′→5′ and synthesises RNA 5′→3′.
  • The genetic code is triplet, degenerate and nearly universal. AUG is the usual initiation codon; UAA, UAG and UGA are stops.
  • In the lac operon, lactose/allolactose removes repressor control; low glucose increases cAMP–CAP activation.
Hardy–Weinberg: p² + 2pq + q² = 1 and p + q = 1
Biology · Human physiology

Circulation

  • SA node is the normal pacemaker. The cardiac cycle includes atrial systole, ventricular systole and joint diastole.
  • Right heart sends deoxygenated blood to lungs; left heart sends oxygenated blood to the body. Human circulation is closed and double.
  • O₂ is carried mainly as oxyhaemoglobin; most CO₂ travels as bicarbonate.

Kidney and hormones

  • Ultrafiltration occurs at the glomerulus. PCT performs bulk reabsorption; loop of Henle contributes to the medullary osmotic gradient.
  • ADH increases water reabsorption in distal nephron/collecting duct. Aldosterone promotes Na⁺ reabsorption and K⁺ secretion.
  • Insulin lowers blood glucose; glucagon raises it. Thyroid hormones support basal metabolism; PTH raises plasma Ca²⁺.

Nervous system

Depolarisation is driven mainly by Na⁺ entry; repolarisation mainly by K⁺ exit. At a chemical synapse, neurotransmitter crosses the cleft in one direction.

Biology · Plant physiology

Transport and photosynthesis

  • Water potential: Ψw = Ψs + Ψp. Pure water has Ψw = 0 under standard conditions; solute potential is negative.
  • Xylem carries water/minerals mainly upward; phloem translocates organic solutes from source to sink.
  • Light reactions occur in thylakoids, producing ATP, NADPH and O₂. The Calvin cycle occurs in stroma.
  • Rubisco fixes CO₂ in C₃ plants. In C₄ plants, PEP carboxylase first fixes CO₂ in mesophyll cells, reducing photorespiration.

Biology · Ecology, evolution & biotech

Ecology

  • Population change: births + immigration increase size; deaths + emigration decrease it.
  • Exponential growth is J-shaped; logistic growth is S-shaped and includes carrying capacity K.
  • Energy flow is unidirectional. The 10% law is a rough ecological transfer rule, not an exact universal constant.
  • Primary succession begins on a lifeless substrate; secondary succession begins where a community previously existed.

Evolution and biotechnology

  • Natural selection acts on heritable variation; populations evolve, not individuals.
  • Homologous structures suggest common ancestry; analogous structures reflect convergent evolution.
  • Restriction endonucleases cut specific DNA sequences; ligase joins fragments; vectors carry inserts into host cells.
  • PCR cycles through denaturation, primer annealing and extension; thermostable DNA polymerase enables repeated cycles.
Chemistry · Physical formulas

Mole, gases and solutions

n = mass / molar mass · N = nNₐ · PV = nRT
Molarity M = moles solute / litres solution · Molality m = moles solute / kg solvent

At fixed temperature for an ideal gas, P ∝ 1/V. Dalton: total pressure equals the sum of partial pressures. Raoult: vapour pressure of a component equals mole fraction × pure-component vapour pressure for an ideal solution.

Thermodynamics and equilibrium

ΔU = q + w · w = −PₑₓₜΔV · ΔG = ΔH − TΔS
ΔG° = −RT ln K · Kp = Kc(RT)^Δn

Spontaneous at constant T,P when ΔG < 0; equilibrium when ΔG = 0. A catalyst changes rate, not K or ΔG°.

Electrochemistry and kinetics

Ecell = Ecathode − Eanode · ΔG° = −nFE°cell
Nernst at 298 K: E = E° − (0.0591/n) log Q
First order: ln([A]₀/[A]t) = kt · t½ = 0.693/k
Chemistry · Atomic structure & bonding

Atomic structure

  • Photon energy: E = hν = hc/λ. de Broglie wavelength: λ = h/mv.
  • For hydrogen-like species: Eₙ = −13.6Z²/n² eV.
  • Maximum electrons in shell n = 2n²; in a subshell = 2(2l+1). Number of orbitals in shell n = n².
  • Pauli: no two electrons in an atom share all four quantum numbers. Hund: maximise unpaired electrons in degenerate orbitals.

Bonding

  • Formal charge = valence electrons − nonbonding electrons − ½(bonding electrons).
  • Greater bond order generally means shorter, stronger bonds.
  • VSEPR counts electron domains: 2 linear, 3 trigonal planar, 4 tetrahedral, 5 trigonal bipyramidal, 6 octahedral before lone-pair distortions.
  • Hybridisation anchors: sp linear; sp² trigonal planar; sp³ tetrahedral electron geometry.
Chemistry · Organic reaction map

Electronic effects

  • Inductive effect operates through σ bonds and decreases with distance. Resonance delocalises electrons in a conjugated system.
  • Carbocation stability is supported by hyperconjugation and electron donation; resonance-stabilised carbocations are especially important.
  • SN1 proceeds through a carbocation and is favoured by stable carbocations and polar protic media. SN2 is one-step backside attack and gives inversion.

Functional-group conversions

Aromatic substitution: activating groups usually direct ortho/para; strongly electron-withdrawing groups usually direct meta, with halogens as the classic deactivating ortho/para exception.

Chemistry · Inorganic memory anchors

Periodic trends

  • Across a period, effective nuclear charge generally rises, atomic radius falls and ionisation enthalpy rises—with known configuration-related exceptions.
  • Down a group, radius generally rises. Cations are smaller than parent atoms; anions are larger.
  • Among isoelectronic species, greater nuclear charge means smaller radius.

Coordination chemistry

  • Oxidation state + ligand charges = complex charge.
  • Coordination number counts donor atoms directly bonded to the metal.
  • Strong-field ligands favour larger splitting and may pair electrons; magnetic moment depends on unpaired electrons.
Spin-only magnetic moment: μ = √[n(n+2)] BM

Physics · Heat, waves & optics

Thermal physics

Q = mcΔT · phase change Q = mL · ideal gas PV = nRT
First law: ΔQ = ΔU + ΔW (when W is work done by system)

For an ideal gas, internal energy depends only on temperature. In an isothermal ideal-gas process ΔU = 0; in an adiabatic process Q = 0.

SHM and waves

a = −ω²x · T spring = 2π√(m/k) · T pendulum ≈ 2π√(l/g)
v = fλ · string wave speed = √(T/μ)

Ray and wave optics

Mirror: 1/f = 1/v + 1/u · Lens: 1/f = 1/v − 1/u (Cartesian sign convention)
Young double slit fringe width β = λD/d

For total internal reflection, light must travel from denser to rarer medium and incidence must exceed the critical angle.

Biology · Living world & classification

Concept chain

  • Taxonomy identifies, names and classifies; systematics also studies evolutionary relationships. The hierarchy runs species → genus → family → order → class → phylum/division → kingdom.
  • A species is a group of closely related organisms capable of interbreeding under natural conditions. Scientific names are binomial: genus capitalised, species lowercase, both italicised.
  • Five-kingdom grouping uses cell structure, body organisation, nutrition, reproduction and phylogeny. Monera are prokaryotic; Protista are primarily unicellular eukaryotes.
  • Viruses are acellular obligate parasites; viroids are infectious naked RNA; lichens are a fungus–alga/cyanobacterium association.

Diagram to rehearse: draw a branching classification key from cell type → organisation → nutrition, then place Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae and Animalia.

Biology · Plant & animal kingdoms

High-yield contrasts

  • Algae: Chlorophyceae store starch, Phaeophyceae laminarin/mannitol, Rhodophyceae floridean starch. Bryophytes need water for fertilisation; pteridophytes are the first vascular cryptogams.
  • Gymnosperms bear naked seeds; angiosperms enclose ovules in ovaries and show double fertilisation.
  • Porifera have canal systems; Cnidaria have cnidoblasts; Platyhelminthes are acoelomate; Aschelminthes pseudocoelomate; Annelida truly coelomate and segmented.
  • Arthropoda have jointed appendages; Mollusca usually possess mantle and radula; Echinoderm adults show radial symmetry and a water vascular system.
  • Chordates possess notochord, dorsal hollow nerve cord, pharyngeal slits and post-anal tail at least embryonically.

Diagram to rehearse: make a ladder of animal phyla annotated with symmetry, coelom, segmentation and one diagnostic organ.

Biology · Morphology & anatomy of plants

Recognition points

  • Tap roots arise from radicle; fibrous roots from stem base; adventitious roots from non-radicle parts. Modifications link form to storage, support or respiration.
  • Leaf phyllotaxy may be alternate, opposite or whorled. Venation is commonly reticulate in dicots and parallel in monocots.
  • Racemose inflorescence keeps the main axis growing; cymose ends in a flower. Floral formulae encode symmetry, sexuality, fusion, ovary position and whorl number.
  • Dicot stem vascular bundles are conjoint, collateral and open; monocot stem bundles are scattered and closed. Dicot root xylem is usually few-armed with small pith; monocot root is polyarch with large pith.

Diagram to rehearse: side-by-side T.S. of dicot root, monocot root, dicot stem and monocot stem; label bundle arrangement, cambium and pith.

Biology · Animal tissues & structural organisation

Core map

  • Simple epithelium permits diffusion/absorption; stratified epithelium protects. Tight, adhering and gap junctions seal, bind and communicate respectively.
  • Connective tissue contains abundant matrix: areolar packs organs, adipose stores fat, tendon joins muscle to bone, ligament joins bone to bone.
  • Skeletal muscle is striated and voluntary; smooth is non-striated and involuntary; cardiac is branched, striated, involuntary and joined by intercalated discs.
  • Earthworm is metamerically segmented, cockroach has open circulation, and frog has a three-chambered heart.

Diagram to rehearse: sketch a neuron and the three muscle types; label cell shape, nuclei, striations and junctions.

Biology · Cell cycle & division

Sequence

  • Interphase comprises G₁ growth, S DNA replication and G₂ preparation. DNA amount doubles in S phase while chromosome number does not.
  • Mitosis preserves chromosome number: prophase condensation, metaphase equatorial alignment, anaphase centromere division, telophase nuclear re-formation.
  • Meiosis I is reductional; homologues pair and separate. Crossing over occurs between non-sister chromatids in pachytene; chiasmata become visible in diplotene.
  • Meiosis II resembles mitosis and separates sister chromatids, producing four haploid cells from one diploid meiocyte.

Diagram to rehearse: track one homologous chromosome pair through mitosis and meiosis, marking DNA content and chromosome number after each division.

Biology · Plant respiration, growth & development

Metabolism

  • Glycolysis occurs in cytoplasm and converts glucose to two pyruvate with net 2 ATP and 2 NADH. Aerobic pyruvate oxidation and Krebs cycle occur in mitochondria.
  • Electron transport on the inner mitochondrial membrane creates the proton gradient used by ATP synthase. Oxygen is the terminal electron acceptor.
  • Respiratory quotient = CO₂ evolved/O₂ consumed: about 1 for carbohydrate, below 1 for fats and above 1 for many organic acids.
  • Growth is irreversible increase; differentiation creates specialised cells, while dedifferentiation and redifferentiation explain cambial activity and secondary tissues.
RQ = volume of CO₂ evolved ÷ volume of O₂ consumed

Diagram to rehearse: map glycolysis → acetyl-CoA → Krebs cycle → electron transport with location and ATP/reducing equivalents.

Biology · Digestion, breathing & gas exchange

Human physiology lines

  • Salivary amylase begins starch digestion; pepsin acts in acidic stomach; pancreatic enzymes and intestinal brush-border enzymes complete digestion.
  • Bile has no digestive enzyme: bile salts emulsify fats and help lipase action. Most nutrient absorption occurs through small-intestinal villi.
  • Inspiration is active: diaphragm contracts and thoracic volume rises. Quiet expiration is mostly passive.
  • O₂ dissociation from haemoglobin increases with high CO₂, high H⁺ and higher temperature. CO₂ is carried mainly as bicarbonate.
Minute ventilation = tidal volume × respiratory rate

Diagram to rehearse: trace food through the alimentary canal and air through a respiratory bronchiole to an alveolus; label exchange surfaces.

Biology · Reproduction in plants & humans

Flower to embryo

  • A typical angiosperm anther has four microsporangia. Pollen mother cells undergo meiosis; the mature pollen commonly has vegetative and generative cells.
  • The usual embryo sac is 7-celled, 8-nucleate. Double fertilisation combines syngamy with triple fusion; endosperm is usually triploid.
  • In humans, spermatogenesis starts at puberty; oogenesis begins before birth and includes long meiotic arrests. Fertilisation usually occurs near the ampullary–isthmic junction.
  • hCG maintains the corpus luteum early in pregnancy; placenta later acts as an endocrine tissue. Parturition involves a positive feedback loop with oxytocin.
  • Contraception may block gamete formation, transport, fertilisation or implantation; STI prevention relies strongly on barrier protection and informed behaviour.

Diagram to rehearse: draw a labelled embryo sac and a menstrual-cycle timeline aligning ovarian events, uterine changes, FSH, LH, estrogen and progesterone.

Biology · Human health, microbes & immunity

Defence and disease

  • Innate immunity is non-specific and includes physical, physiological, cellular and cytokine barriers. Acquired immunity is antigen-specific and develops memory.
  • B cells produce antibodies; cell-mediated immunity is chiefly T-cell driven. Vaccination induces active immunity; ready-made antibodies give passive immunity.
  • HIV targets helper T cells; malaria is caused by Plasmodium and transmitted by female Anopheles; typhoid is bacterial and Widal is a diagnostic test.
  • Lactobacillus helps curd formation; methanogens aid biogas; microbes produce antibiotics, organic acids, enzymes and sewage-treatment flocs.
  • Cancer involves uncontrolled cell division; metastasis is spread to distant sites. Carcinogens may be physical, chemical or biological.

Diagram to rehearse: flowchart primary and secondary immune responses, and the sewage-treatment stages from screening to anaerobic sludge digestion.

Chemistry · Some basic concepts

Numerical foundation

  • Moles connect microscopic count, mass and gas volume. Use dimensional analysis before substitution.
  • Empirical formula gives the simplest ratio; molecular formula is an integer multiple fixed by molar mass.
  • The limiting reagent is consumed first and fixes theoretical yield. Percentage yield = actual/theoretical × 100.
  • Significant figures follow measurement precision; exact counted numbers do not limit them.
n = m/M · N = nNₐ · mass % = component mass/total mass × 100

Diagram to rehearse: a conversion triangle linking mass, moles and particles, with one worked dimensional-analysis chain.

Chemistry · Periodicity & chemical bonding

Cause before trend

  • Periodicity follows repeating valence-shell configurations. Shielding and effective nuclear charge explain radius and ionisation trends.
  • Electron gain enthalpy and electronegativity are not identical; noble gases and stable half/full subshells create exceptions.
  • Ionic bonding is electrostatic and favoured by suitable ionisation enthalpy, electron gain and lattice enthalpy. Covalent character increases with polarisation.
  • Bond order from MO theory = ½(bonding electrons − antibonding electrons). Unpaired electrons predict paramagnetism.
  • Lone pairs compress bond angles more strongly than bond pairs: LP–LP > LP–BP > BP–BP repulsion.
Bond order = (Nᵦ − Nₐ)/2 · dipole moment μ = q × r

Diagram to rehearse: VSEPR shapes and MO filling for B₂, C₂, N₂ and O₂, including magnetic behaviour.

Chemistry · Thermodynamics & equilibrium

Direction and extent

  • State functions depend only on endpoints; heat and work depend on path. At constant volume qᵥ = ΔU; at constant pressure qₚ = ΔH.
  • Hess’s law works because enthalpy is a state function. Bond enthalpy estimates use bonds broken minus bonds formed.
  • Dynamic equilibrium has equal forward and reverse rates, not equal concentrations. K includes equilibrium activities/concentrations only for species included by convention.
  • Le Chatelier predicts the direction of shift after a disturbance; temperature alone changes K.
  • For acids, pH = −log[H⁺]. A buffer resists pH change using a weak acid/base and its conjugate partner.
ΔH = ΔU + ΔnᵍRT · ΔG = ΔH − TΔS · pH + pOH = 14 at 298 K

Diagram to rehearse: reaction-coordinate curves with and without catalyst, marking Eₐ and unchanged ΔH.

Chemistry · Redox, solutions & colligative properties

Particles and electrons

  • Oxidation raises oxidation number; reduction lowers it. In disproportionation the same species is both oxidised and reduced.
  • Balance redox equations by electron or ion–electron method; verify both charge and atom balance.
  • Ideal solutions obey Raoult’s law across composition and have ΔHmix = 0 and ΔVmix = 0. Positive deviation weakens unlike interactions.
  • Colligative properties depend on number of solute particles. van ’t Hoff factor corrects for association or dissociation.
pᵢ = xᵢpᵢ° · ΔTᵦ = iKᵦm · ΔT𝒻 = iK𝒻m · π = iCRT

Diagram to rehearse: vapour-pressure/composition curves for ideal, positive-deviation and negative-deviation solutions.

Chemistry · Electrochemistry & kinetics

Cells and rates

  • Oxidation occurs at anode and reduction at cathode in both cell types. In a galvanic cell electrons flow externally from anode to cathode.
  • Salt bridge maintains electroneutrality and completes the circuit. A positive Ecell means spontaneous operation as written under stated conditions.
  • Molar conductivity rises on dilution; the increase is steep for weak electrolytes because ionisation also rises.
  • Rate law is experimental. Molecularity belongs to an elementary step; order is the sum of rate-law exponents and may be zero or fractional.
  • Arrhenius behaviour links rate constant to temperature and activation energy.
E = E° − (0.0591/n)logQ · Λₘ = κ×1000/C · k = Ae^(−Eₐ/RT)

Diagram to rehearse: Daniell cell with ion/electron directions, and concentration–time curves for zero- and first-order reactions.

Chemistry · p-block, d-block & f-block

Textbook anchors

  • First members of p-block groups are anomalous because of small size, high electronegativity and absence of d orbitals.
  • Inert-pair effect makes lower oxidation states more stable down heavier p-block groups.
  • Transition elements show variable oxidation states, coloured ions, complex formation and catalysis because d orbitals are involved.
  • Zn, Cd and Hg are not transition elements by the strict incomplete-d-subshell definition in atoms/common ions.
  • Lanthanoid contraction arises from poor 4f shielding; it causes similar sizes of second- and third-row transition pairs.

Diagram to rehearse: oxidation-state ladders for N, P, S, halogens, Mn and Cr; annotate oxidising/reducing tendencies.

Chemistry · Coordination compounds

Language and geometry

  • Write cation before anion; ligands are named before metal. Anionic complex metal names often end in “-ate”.
  • Coordination number counts donor atoms, so a bidentate ligand contributes two. Denticity is not ligand charge.
  • Structural isomerism includes ionisation, hydrate/solvate, linkage and coordination forms; stereoisomerism includes geometrical and optical forms.
  • Crystal-field splitting differs for octahedral and tetrahedral fields; pairing depends on Δ versus pairing energy.
Effective atomic number = metal Z − oxidation state + electrons donated by ligands

Diagram to rehearse: d-orbital splitting in octahedral and tetrahedral fields plus cis/trans square-planar isomers.

Chemistry · Organic functional groups

Mechanism map

  • Alkenes undergo electrophilic addition; Markovnikov orientation follows the more stable intermediate, while peroxide effect is characteristic for HBr.
  • Haloalkanes show substitution/elimination competition. Aryl halides resist ordinary nucleophilic substitution because the C–X bond has partial double-bond character.
  • Phenol is more acidic than alcohol because phenoxide is resonance-stabilised. Aldehydes are generally more reactive than ketones toward nucleophilic addition.
  • Carboxylic acids form resonance-stabilised carboxylates. Amines are bases/nucleophiles; aromatic amines are less basic than aliphatic amines due to lone-pair delocalisation.
  • Use reagent, substrate and condition together; the same reagent can give different products under different conditions.

Diagram to rehearse: one conversion web joining alkene, haloalkane, alcohol, carbonyl, acid, ester and amine with reagents over arrows.

Chemistry · Biomolecules & practical chemistry

Recognition and tests

  • Glucose is an aldohexose; fructose is a ketohexose. Sucrose is non-reducing, while maltose and lactose are reducing disaccharides.
  • Proteins are polypeptides; denaturation disrupts higher structure without normally breaking primary peptide bonds.
  • DNA usually has deoxyribose and thymine; RNA has ribose and uracil. Complementary pairing supports faithful replication.
  • Lassaigne’s test converts covalently bound N, S or halogen into ionic salts. Chromatography separates by differential distribution.
  • Functional-group tests must be read as observation plus inference: precipitate colour, gas evolution, odour or decolourisation.

Diagram to rehearse: paper-chromatography setup and a decision tree for unsaturation, carbonyl, carboxyl and amine tests.

Physics · Units, vectors & kinematics

Set up correctly

  • Dimensions test consistency but cannot supply numerical constants. Percentage uncertainty in products/powers adds with absolute power multipliers.
  • Resolve vectors along convenient axes; dot product gives a scalar and cross product a perpendicular vector.
  • Slope of x–t is velocity; slope of v–t is acceleration; area under v–t is displacement.
  • Relative velocity of A with respect to B is v⃗A − v⃗B. Projectile horizontal and vertical motions share time but otherwise evolve independently.
[G] = M⁻¹L³T⁻² · v⃗AB = v⃗A − v⃗B · a = dv/dt

Diagram to rehearse: x–t, v–t and a–t graphs for rest, uniform motion and uniform acceleration; label slopes and areas.

Physics · Laws of motion, work & energy

Free-body logic

  • Inertia belongs to a body; force changes momentum. Action and reaction act on different bodies and never cancel on one free-body diagram.
  • Friction opposes relative motion or its tendency. Static friction adjusts up to μsN; kinetic friction is approximately μkN.
  • Net work equals change in kinetic energy. Conservative-force work is path-independent and equals −ΔU.
  • Mechanical energy is conserved when only conservative forces do work. Power is the rate of work and equals F⃗·v⃗ instantaneously.
F⃗net = dp⃗/dt · Wnet = ΔK · P = dW/dt = F⃗·v⃗

Diagram to rehearse: inclined-block free-body diagrams for rest, impending slide and acceleration, with axes along/perpendicular to plane.

Physics · Rotation & gravitation

Angular analogies

  • Torque changes angular momentum. With zero external torque, total angular momentum is conserved.
  • Moment of inertia depends on mass distribution and axis. Parallel-axis theorem: I = Icm + Md².
  • For rolling without slipping vcm = Rω; total kinetic energy has translation plus rotation.
  • Gravitational field and potential are vector/scalar respectively; potential is negative if zero is at infinity.
  • Satellites in lower circular orbits move faster; escape speed is √2 times circular-orbit speed at the same radius.
τ⃗ = dL⃗/dt · K = ½Mv² + ½Icmω² · vₒ = √(GM/r)

Diagram to rehearse: rolling body contact forces and radial graphs of g and gravitational potential inside/outside a uniform sphere.

Physics · Solids, fluids & surface phenomena

Material response

  • Stress is restoring force per area; strain is fractional deformation. Hooke’s law holds only in the linear elastic region.
  • Pressure at depth is P₀ + ρgh. Pascal’s law transmits pressure; Archimedes’ buoyant force equals displaced-fluid weight.
  • Continuity expresses mass conservation; Bernoulli connects pressure, speed and height along steady, incompressible, non-viscous flow.
  • Surface tension acts tangentially and tends to minimise area. Excess pressure is 2T/r for a liquid drop and 4T/r for a soap bubble.
Y = longitudinal stress/strain · A₁v₁ = A₂v₂ · P + ½ρv² + ρgh = constant

Diagram to rehearse: stress–strain curve with proportional, elastic, yield and fracture regions; Venturi tube pressure/speed labels.

Physics · Thermal physics & kinetic theory

Microscopic to macroscopic

  • Temperature measures thermal state; heat is energy transfer. During a phase change at fixed pressure, temperature stays constant while latent heat is exchanged.
  • Expansion: ΔL = αLΔT; area and volume coefficients are approximately 2α and 3α for isotropic solids.
  • For ideal gases, internal energy depends only on temperature. Cp − Cv = R per mole and γ = Cp/Cv.
  • RMS molecular speed grows as √T and falls as 1/√M. Mean translational kinetic energy per molecule is 3kT/2.
vᵣₘₛ = √(3RT/M) · U = (f/2)nRT · PVᵞ = constant for reversible adiabatic change

Diagram to rehearse: P–V curves for isothermal and adiabatic expansion, shading work and comparing steepness.

Physics · Oscillations & waves

Periodicity

  • SHM requires restoring acceleration proportional and opposite to displacement. Speed is maximum at mean position; acceleration magnitude at extremes.
  • Total SHM energy remains constant; kinetic and potential energies exchange, each oscillating at twice the displacement frequency.
  • A travelling wave transfers energy without net bulk transport of medium. Phase difference = 2πΔx/λ.
  • Standing waves have nodes and antinodes; adjacent nodes are λ/2 apart. Beats occur when nearby frequencies superpose.
x = A cos(ωt+φ) · v = ±ω√(A²−x²) · fbeat = |f₁−f₂|

Diagram to rehearse: x, v and a versus time for SHM, plus first three harmonics of a string and an open/closed pipe.

Physics · Electrostatics, capacitance & current

Fields to circuits

  • Electric field is force per positive test charge; field lines start on positive and end on negative charge. Electrostatic field is conservative.
  • Gauss’s law is most useful with high symmetry. Inside a conductor in electrostatic equilibrium E = 0 and excess charge lies on the surface.
  • Capacitors in parallel add directly; series reciprocals add. A dielectric increases capacitance by factor K when geometry is fixed.
  • Drift speed is small although circuit response is rapid. Resistivity depends on material and temperature, not sample dimensions.
  • Kirchhoff junction and loop rules express charge and energy conservation.
∮E⃗·dA⃗ = qenc/ε₀ · Cseries⁻¹ = ΣCᵢ⁻¹ · I = neAvd

Diagram to rehearse: field/equipotential lines for a dipole, capacitor series/parallel networks and a labelled Wheatstone bridge.

Physics · Magnetism, EMI & alternating current

Direction and induction

  • Magnetic force qvB sinθ changes direction, not speed, of a charged particle. A current loop behaves as magnetic dipole m = IA.
  • Biot–Savart and Ampère laws calculate B from currents; symmetry decides which is convenient.
  • Faraday’s law gives induced emf from changing flux; Lenz’s law sets direction opposing the flux change.
  • In a pure resistor V and I are in phase; in pure inductor current lags by 90°; in pure capacitor current leads by 90°.
  • At series LCR resonance XL = XC, impedance is minimum and current maximum.
Bwire = μ₀I/2πr · ε = −N dΦ/dt · XL = ωL · XC = 1/ωC

Diagram to rehearse: right-hand-rule fields, motional-emf rod, and R/L/C phasor diagrams with the resonance triangle.

Physics · Optics, modern physics & electronics

Light to devices

  • Use one Cartesian sign convention consistently. Magnification sign distinguishes erect/inverted images; ray diagrams should verify algebra.
  • Interference redistributes intensity; diffraction becomes important when aperture size is comparable to wavelength. Polarisation establishes transverse character.
  • Photoelectric threshold depends on material. Increasing frequency raises Kmax; increasing intensity raises saturation current above threshold.
  • Bohr quantisation explains hydrogenic levels; line emission occurs when electrons fall to lower energy. Nuclear binding energy equals mass defect × c².
  • Intrinsic semiconductors have equal electron/hole densities. Doping creates n-type or p-type material; a p–n junction conducts strongly in forward bias.
Lens maker: 1/f = (μ−1)(1/R₁−1/R₂) · Eₙ = −13.6Z²/n² eV · BE = Δmc²

Diagram to rehearse: principal rays for mirrors/lenses, photoelectric I–V curves, hydrogen levels and p–n junction biasing.

Practice

Practice with explanations

Sixty-question concept bank

Twenty questions per subject, spread across the syllabus. Commit to an option first, then open the answer for the governing idea and calculation.

Biology · Cell

1. Which organelle is the principal site of packaging and sorting of proteins received from the ER?

A. LysosomeB. Golgi apparatusC. PeroxisomeD. Nucleolus
Show answer

B. Proteins from the ER enter the cis face of the Golgi, are modified and sorted through cisternae, and leave from the trans face in vesicles.

Biology · Genetics

2. In a Hardy–Weinberg population, the recessive phenotype frequency is 0.16. What is the heterozygote frequency?

A. 0.16B. 0.24C. 0.48D. 0.64
Show answer

C. q² = 0.16, so q = 0.4 and p = 0.6. Heterozygotes = 2pq = 2 × 0.6 × 0.4 = 0.48.

Biology · Photosynthesis

3. The first stable CO₂-fixation product in C₄ plants is:

A. 3-PGAB. Oxaloacetic acidC. RuBPD. Pyruvic acid
Show answer

B. PEP carboxylase fixes bicarbonate in mesophyll cells to form the four-carbon oxaloacetate. C₃ plants form 3-PGA first.

Biology · Human physiology

4. Which hormone most directly increases water permeability of the collecting duct?

A. InsulinB. ThyroxineC. ADHD. Glucagon
Show answer

C. ADH promotes insertion of aquaporin water channels, increasing water reabsorption and concentrating urine.

Biology · Molecular basis

5. Which sequence lists the standard stop codons?

A. AUG, UGG, UGAB. UAA, UAG, UGAC. UUU, UUC, UUAD. AUG, GUG, UUG
Show answer

B. UAA, UAG and UGA terminate translation; they do not specify amino acids in the standard genetic code.

Chemistry · Mole concept

6. How many molecules are present in 0.50 mol of an ideal molecular substance?

A. 0.5B. 3.011 × 10²³C. 6.022 × 10²³D. 12.044 × 10²³
Show answer

B. Number = nNₐ = 0.50 × 6.022 × 10²³ = 3.011 × 10²³ molecules.

Chemistry · Equilibrium

7. Adding a catalyst to a system already at equilibrium will:

A. Increase KB. Shift equilibrium rightC. Shift equilibrium leftD. Leave composition and K unchanged
Show answer

D. A catalyst lowers activation energy for forward and reverse reactions, helping equilibrium establish faster without changing K or equilibrium composition.

Chemistry · Kinetics

8. For a first-order reaction, the half-life is:

A. Proportional to initial concentrationB. Independent of initial concentrationC. Inversely proportional to concentration squaredD. Always zero
Show answer

B. t½ = 0.693/k for a first-order reaction, so it does not contain initial concentration.

Chemistry · Bonding

9. Which species is smallest in the isoelectronic series O²⁻, F⁻, Na⁺ and Mg²⁺?

A. O²⁻B. F⁻C. Na⁺D. Mg²⁺
Show answer

D. All have 10 electrons. Mg²⁺ has the greatest nuclear charge, pulling the same electron cloud most strongly and giving the smallest radius.

Chemistry · Organic

10. An SN2 reaction at a chiral carbon characteristically produces:

A. Racemisation onlyB. Retention onlyC. Inversion of configurationD. No bond breaking
Show answer

C. The nucleophile attacks from the side opposite the leaving group in one concerted step, producing Walden inversion.

Physics · Kinematics

11. A body starts from rest with constant acceleration 2 m s⁻². Its speed after 5 s is:

A. 2 m s⁻¹B. 5 m s⁻¹C. 10 m s⁻¹D. 25 m s⁻¹
Show answer

C. v = u + at = 0 + 2 × 5 = 10 m s⁻¹.

Physics · Energy

12. If the speed of a body doubles, its kinetic energy becomes:

A. HalfB. DoubleC. Four timesD. Eight times
Show answer

C. K = ½mv². Replacing v with 2v multiplies kinetic energy by 2² = 4.

Physics · Current electricity

13. Two resistors 6 Ω and 3 Ω are connected in parallel. Their equivalent resistance is:

A. 1 ΩB. 2 ΩC. 4.5 ΩD. 9 Ω
Show answer

B. 1/R = 1/6 + 1/3 = 3/6 = 1/2, so R = 2 Ω.

Physics · Optics

14. In Young’s double-slit experiment, doubling slit separation while other quantities stay fixed makes fringe width:

A. DoubleB. HalfC. Four timesD. Unchanged
Show answer

B. Fringe width β = λD/d, so doubling d halves β.

Physics · Modern physics

15. Above threshold frequency, increasing light intensity at fixed frequency primarily increases:

A. Maximum kinetic energyB. Stopping potentialC. Number of emitted photoelectronsD. Work function
Show answer

C. Higher intensity delivers more photons per unit time and therefore can eject more electrons. Kmax depends on frequency through hν − φ.

Mock-test discipline

Scores improve after the test

Attempting papers reveals problems. Analysis fixes them.

Use the official marking logic

Score = 4C − W

C = correct responses, W = wrong responses. Unanswered questions contribute zero. Track accuracy separately: correct ÷ attempted × 100.

Attempt order: secure questions → moderate questions → flagged questions. Do not let one calculation consume a cluster of easier marks.

The 30-minute post-test audit

  1. Separate wrong answers from lucky guesses.
  2. Label cause: concept, recall, calculation, misread, time or over-attempt.
  3. Write one corrective action per repeatable error.
  4. Re-solve without the key within 48 hours.
  5. Retest the same concept in a mixed set after seven days.

The daily engine
Every chapter must pass through five gates.

Watching a lecture is not completion. A chapter becomes usable only after recall, questions and scheduled revision.

Learn the concept

Use one teacher or source. Stop switching. Write only definitions, diagrams, derivations and exceptions that you will revise.

Read the source

For Biology and Inorganic Chemistry, make a second pass through NCERT. Mark unfamiliar lines—not entire pages.

Solve immediately

Do 40–80 chapter MCQs without looking at notes. Mark each miss as concept, recall, calculation, reading or guess.

Revisit the misses

Correct the idea, not just the answer. Re-solve wrong and guessed questions after 48 hours and again after one week.

Mix and time

Combine old and new chapters. Progress from chapter tests to unit tests, part syllabus and full OMR simulations.
