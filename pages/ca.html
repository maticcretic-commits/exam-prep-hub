Study Material

Foundation · Accounting

Accounting mechanics

  • Every transaction preserves the equation: Assets = Equity + Liabilities.
  • Debit increases assets/expenses; credit increases capital, liabilities and income.
  • A trial balance checks arithmetic equality, not complete accuracy: omission and principle errors may remain.
  • Depreciable amount is cost less residual value; match the method to the asset's consumption pattern.
SLM depreciation = (Cost − Residual value) ÷ Useful life
Foundation · Business Laws

Contract answer framework

  • A valid agreement needs competent parties, free consent, lawful consideration and lawful object.
  • An offer must be communicated; acceptance must be absolute and correspond to the offer.
  • Distinguish void, voidable, illegal and unenforceable agreements before discussing remedy.
  • Write answers as Issue → Rule → Apply facts → Conclusion.
Foundation · Business Mathematics

Time value and ratios

  • Keep rate and time in the same period before using an interest formula.
  • A ratio compares like quantities; a proportion states equality of two ratios.
  • For annuities, identify whether cash flow occurs at the end or beginning of each period.
Amount (compound) = P(1 + r)ⁿ · PV = FV ÷ (1 + r)ⁿ
Foundation · Statistics

Central tendency and spread

  • Mean uses every value but is sensitive to extremes; median is positional and robust to extremes.
  • Variance is the mean squared deviation; standard deviation restores the original unit.
  • Correlation measures association, not causation. Its coefficient lies between −1 and +1.
σ = √[Σ(x − x̄)² ÷ N] · CV = (σ ÷ x̄) × 100
Foundation · Economics

Demand, supply and elasticity

  • A movement along demand follows own-price change; a shift follows income, tastes, expectations or related-goods prices.
  • Elastic demand has |Ed| > 1; inelastic demand has |Ed| < 1.
  • Opportunity cost is the value of the next-best alternative forgone.
  • Marginal thinking compares the extra benefit and extra cost of one more unit.
Price elasticity of demand = %Δ quantity demanded ÷ %Δ price
Intermediate · Advanced Accounting

Standard-led problem solving

  • Start by identifying the recognition question, measurement basis and presentation/disclosure impact.
  • Inventory is generally carried at the lower of cost and net realisable value.
  • Cash flow classification separates operating, investing and financing activities.
  • Show adjustment entries and working notes before the final statement.
NRV = Estimated selling price − Completion cost − Selling cost
Intermediate · Corporate & Other Laws

Provision-to-facts method

  • First identify entity, actor, power, condition, time limit and consequence.
  • Separate the general rule from exceptions and provisos.
  • Do not invent section numbers; accurate principle and application beat a wrong citation.
  • Conclude only after applying each decisive fact to the rule.
Intermediate · Taxation

Tax computation discipline

  • Income-tax flow: scope → residential status → heads of income → set-off → deductions → tax liability.
  • GST flow: supply → time → place → value → rate → input tax credit → net liability.
  • Build a separate amendment sheet for the exact attempt; never mix assessment years.
  • State assumptions when facts are incomplete.
Net GST payable = Output tax liability − Eligible input tax credit
Intermediate · Costing

Decision formulas

  • Contribution first covers fixed cost; the remainder is profit.
  • Relevant cost changes between alternatives and relates to the future.
  • Standard costing separates price/rate effects from quantity/efficiency effects.
  • Reconcile cost and financial profits systematically, not from memory.
Contribution = Sales − Variable cost · P/V ratio = Contribution ÷ Sales · BEP sales = Fixed cost ÷ P/V ratio
Intermediate · Auditing & Ethics

Risk and evidence

  • Management assertions guide procedures: existence, completeness, rights/obligations, valuation and presentation.
  • Audit evidence must be sufficient (quantity) and appropriate (quality).
  • Professional scepticism means a questioning mind and alertness to contradiction.
  • Link procedure to risk: inspect, observe, confirm, recalculate, reperform, inquire or analyse.
Audit risk = Inherent risk × Control risk × Detection risk
Intermediate · FM & Strategy

Value and strategic fit

  • Accept a conventional project when NPV is positive at the required return, subject to constraints and qualitative factors.
  • WACC uses market-value weights where appropriate and an after-tax cost of debt.
  • Strategy links external opportunity/threat with internal strength/weakness and execution capability.
  • A ratio is a prompt for investigation, not a conclusion by itself.
NPV = Σ[Cash flowₜ ÷ (1+k)ᵗ] − Initial outlay
Final · Financial Reporting

Recognition before calculation

  • Identify control, significant influence or a simple financial-asset relationship before choosing accounting treatment.
  • For financial instruments, classification drives subsequent measurement and impairment treatment.
  • Revenue analysis starts with contract, performance obligations and timing of satisfaction.
  • In consolidation, eliminate intragroup balances and unrealised profits consistently.
Final · Advanced FM

Valuation and risk

  • Discount cash flows matching the claim: FCFF with WACC, FCFE with cost of equity.
  • A hedge reduces exposure; it does not need to create a speculative gain.
  • Separate operating synergy from financing effects in acquisition valuation.
  • State quotation convention and timeline before a forex calculation.
FCFF = EBIT(1−T) + Depreciation − Capex − ΔWorking capital
Final · Advanced Audit

Report from evidence

  • Move from identified risk to response, evidence, conclusion and reporting implication.
  • Material misstatement can arise from error or fraud; fraud adds incentives, opportunity and rationalisation concerns.
  • Going concern assessment and auditor reporting are related but distinct decisions.
  • Ethics answers should identify threat, significance, safeguards and whether the engagement can continue.
Final · Direct & International Tax

Cross-border framework

  • Classify the taxpayer and transaction before applying domestic charging provisions.
  • Then test treaty access, permanent establishment and allocation rules where relevant.
  • Transfer pricing focuses on the arm's-length outcome between associated enterprises.
  • Document assumptions and apply only provisions relevant to the notified attempt.
Final · Indirect Tax

Transaction map

  • Resolve classification and supply first; valuation and rate follow that answer.
  • Time of supply identifies when liability arises; place of supply helps determine tax type and jurisdiction.
  • Input tax credit requires eligibility, documentation and absence of a blocked-credit rule.
  • Customs problems need assessable value, exchange-rate/date rules and the correct duty sequence.
Final · Integrated Business Solutions

Open-book case method

  • Build a slim topic index before the exam; searching from scratch consumes time.
  • Read the requirement before the case, then tag facts by finance, tax, law, risk and strategy.
  • Prioritise recommendation and reasoning over copying theory.
  • State commercial trade-offs, assumptions and implementation risks.
Final · SPOM

Four modules, four qualifications

  • Set A: Corporate & Economic Laws; Set B: Strategic Cost & Performance Management.
  • Set C: choose one elective; Set D: choose one multidisciplinary subject.
  • Finish required learning hours before the module test.
  • Treat SPOMs as part of Final eligibility, not as a post-exam formality.
Intermediate · FM

Core formula sheet

  • NPV = PV of inflows − initial outlay; accept when NPV > 0.
  • IRR: discount rate at which NPV = 0; accept when IRR > cost of capital.
  • WACC = Σ(weight × after-tax cost) across debt and equity.
  • DOL = %ΔEBIT ÷ %ΔSales; DFL = %ΔEPS ÷ %ΔEBIT; DCL = DOL × DFL.
  • EOQ = √(2 × annual demand × order cost ÷ carrying cost per unit).
  • Current ratio = CA ÷ CL; quick ratio = (CA − inventory) ÷ CL; debt-equity = long-term debt ÷ equity.
Intermediate · Taxation

Income-tax computation order

  • Step 1: residential status (182-day test; 60 + 365-day test; additional conditions).
  • Step 2: classify every income into the five heads: salary, house property, PGBP, capital gains, other sources.
  • Step 3: aggregate to gross total income after intra-head and inter-head set-off rules.
  • Step 4: deduct Chapter VI-A deductions (80C–80U) — availability differs between old and new regimes.
  • Step 5: apply slab rates, rebate u/s 87A, surcharge, then 4% health & education cess.
Intermediate · Audit

Standards numbers that matter

  • SA 200: overall objectives; SA 230: audit documentation.
  • SA 315: identify and assess risks of material misstatement.
  • SA 320: materiality; SA 330: responses to assessed risks.
  • SA 500: audit evidence; SA 700 series: forming an opinion and reporting.
  • Management assertions: Completeness, Accuracy, Existence, Rights & obligations, Valuation, Presentation.
Final · FR

High-frequency Ind AS

  • Ind AS 1 presentation; Ind AS 2 inventories; Ind AS 7 cash flows; Ind AS 16 property, plant & equipment.
  • Ind AS 33 EPS; Ind AS 36 impairment; Ind AS 37 provisions; Ind AS 38 intangibles.
  • Ind AS 103 business combinations; Ind AS 105 held for sale; Ind AS 109 financial instruments.
  • Ind AS 115 revenue from contracts; Ind AS 116 leases.
Intermediate · Costing

Marginal costing formats

  • P/V ratio = Contribution ÷ Sales × 100.
  • Break-even sales = Fixed cost ÷ P/V ratio.
  • Margin of safety = actual sales − break-even sales.
  • With a limiting (key) factor, rank products by contribution per unit of that factor.
  • Make-or-buy: compare differential cost with the purchase price, not total cost.
Foundation · Accounting

Depreciation and final-accounts adjustments

  • Straight-line: (cost − residual value) ÷ useful life, same charge every year.
  • Written-down value: rate × opening book value; charge falls each year.
  • Outstanding expenses add to the expense and appear as a current liability.
  • Prepaid expenses reduce the expense and appear as a current asset.
  • Closing stock reduces cost of goods sold and appears as a current asset.

Practice

Practice with explanations — 20 questions

Try each question on paper before opening the answer. The explanation shows the reasoning — not just the result.

1. A machine costs ₹5,00,000, has a ₹50,000 residual value and a 5-year useful life. What is annual straight-line depreciation?
₹90,000. Depreciable amount = ₹5,00,000 − ₹50,000 = ₹4,50,000. Divide by five years: ₹90,000 per year.
2. Why can a trial balance agree even when the accounts contain an error?
Because it tests debit-credit equality, not complete correctness. A complete omission, an equal error on both sides, or posting to the wrong account of the same type can leave total debits equal to total credits.
3. Price rises by 10% and quantity demanded falls by 20%. What is the magnitude of price elasticity?
2, so demand is elastic. |Ed| = 20% ÷ 10% = 2. The negative direction is understood from the law of demand; classification usually uses the magnitude.
4. A product sells for ₹500 with variable cost of ₹300. Fixed cost is ₹4,00,000. What is the break-even volume?
2,000 units. Contribution per unit = ₹500 − ₹300 = ₹200. Break-even units = ₹4,00,000 ÷ ₹200.
5. What is the strongest first step in a business-law case answer?
Identify the legal issue from the facts. Then state the applicable rule, apply decisive facts and conclude. Starting with unrelated theory wastes time and can obscure the issue.
6. If output GST is ₹1,20,000 and eligible input tax credit is ₹85,000, what is the simple net liability before interest or other adjustments?
₹35,000. Net liability = output tax liability − eligible input tax credit. Eligibility must be tested before taking the credit.
7. What makes audit evidence “appropriate”?
Its relevance and reliability. Sufficiency refers to quantity. Appropriateness refers to quality—whether the evidence supports the assertion and how dependable its source and nature are.
8. A project costs ₹1,00,000 and the present value of expected cash inflows is ₹1,18,000. What is its NPV and basic decision?
NPV is ₹18,000; accept in the basic unconstrained case. NPV = ₹1,18,000 − ₹1,00,000. A positive NPV indicates value above the required return, though constraints and non-financial risks still matter.
9. Why should FCFF normally be discounted at WACC rather than only the cost of equity?
FCFF belongs to all capital providers. WACC reflects the required returns of debt and equity in their capital weights. FCFE, which belongs only to equity holders, is discounted at cost of equity.
10. In an open-book case paper, why is a prepared index more valuable than carrying more material?
Because retrieval time is the constraint. A compact index turns material into usable evidence quickly; unindexed volumes can consume the time needed for analysis and writing.
11. What is the minimum pass rule common to Foundation and to each Intermediate/Final group?
At least 40% in each paper and 50% in aggregate. Intermediate and Final also contain group-specific rules and exemptions, so verify your exact situation in ICAI's current FAQ.
12. A student scored 60% in an Intermediate paper but did not pass the group. What should they check?
Whether the ICAI paper exemption applies. Under the current FAQ, 60% or more can create an exemption for the next three examinations when the student appeared in all papers of the group; aggregate requirements still apply.
13. Average maintainable profit is ₹2,00,000 and goodwill is valued at 3 years’ purchase. What is goodwill?
₹6,00,000. Goodwill = average maintainable profit × years’ purchase = ₹2,00,000 × 3.
14. A agrees to pay B ₹10,000 purely for “moral support” during a dispute. Is the promise enforceable?
No. A contract needs lawful consideration — something of value exchanged. A promise based only on moral obligation, without real consideration, is generally unenforceable.
15. A correlation coefficient of +0.9 between advertising spend and sales suggests what?
A strong positive linear relationship. Values near +1 mean the variables move together closely; correlation alone does not prove causation.
16. An individual is present in India for 200 days in the previous year. What is the basic residential-status result?
Resident. The 182-day test is satisfied. Whether ordinarily or not-ordinarily resident depends on the additional conditions, which must still be checked.
17. Which Standard on Auditing deals with identifying and assessing the risks of material misstatement?
SA 315. It requires the auditor to understand the entity and its environment and to identify and assess risks of material misstatement at the financial-statement and assertion levels.
18. A project costs ₹1,00,000 today; inflows are ₹60,000 in year 1 and ₹60,000 in year 2. At 10%%, is the NPV positive?
Yes — about ₹4,130. PV = 60,000 ÷ 1.1 + 60,000 ÷ 1.21 = 54,545 + 49,587 = 1,04,132. NPV = 1,04,132 − 1,00,000 = +₹4,132. A positive NPV supports acceptance.
19. A parent holds 80%% of a subsidiary that earned ₹10,00,000 profit for the year. What is the non-controlling interest’s share?
₹2,00,000. The NCI holds 20%%, so 20%% of ₹10,00,000 = ₹2,00,000 is attributed to the non-controlling interest.
20. Under the current scheme, how many SPOM sets must a Final student clear?
Four — Sets A, B, C and D. A: Corporate & Economic Laws; B: Strategic Cost & Performance Management; C: one elective; D: one multidisciplinary subject. Check ICAI for attempt-specific learning-hour rules.

Study Plan

A study loop that survives a long course

01

Learn

Read the ICAI chapter and class notes. Mark illustrations, provisions, formats and exceptions—not entire pages.

02

Recall

Close the book. Rebuild the formula, provision, audit procedure or answer skeleton from memory.

03

Apply

Solve ICAI illustrations, module questions, MCQs and past questions. Maintain one error log.

04

Simulate

Write timed sectional tests, then full MTP/RTP-style papers. Review presentation and unfinished questions.

60 minPractice: questions without watching solutions first
45 minSecond subject: law/audit/economics active recall
30 minRevision: yesterday + 7-day-old topics
15 minError log: record why the mistake happened and the correction rule

Weekly non-negotiables

  • ✓One cumulative test under a fixed timer
  • ✓One amendment/applicability check for tax and law
  • ✓One written-answer review for structure and keywords
  • ✓One backlog decision: schedule, shrink or drop
  • ✓One half-day with no new content—revision only

The 12-week revision sprint

Weeks 1–4

Close conceptual gaps

Map every chapter as green, amber or red. Relearn red chapters from ICAI material; solve representative questions immediately.

Weeks 5–8

Convert coverage into recall

Run mixed chapter tests, compress notes, revisit the error log and practise provisions/formulas without prompts.

Weeks 9–10

Full-paper simulation

Alternate full papers and review days. Track attempted marks, time leakage, presentation and avoidable errors.

Week 11

Patch weak zones

Use test evidence—not anxiety—to choose chapters. Redo incorrect questions and rehearse high-frequency formats.

Week 12

Stabilise, don't expand

Revision notes, amendments, key illustrations and one light simulation. No new teacher, book or question bank.

Every Sunday

Audit the system

Compare planned versus completed hours, update the next seven days, and protect sleep before adding workload.

Protect marks you already know

Stop doing this

  • ×Collecting multiple books before finishing one ICAI module
  • ×Watching solutions before making a real attempt
  • ×Leaving law/audit writing practice for the final month
  • ×Studying tax without an attempt-specific amendment sheet
  • ×Treating an open-book paper as an unprepared paper

Do this instead

  • ✓Choose questions in the reading window; set time checkpoints
  • ✓Show formulas, assumptions and working notes clearly
  • ✓Use headings and short paragraphs for law and audit answers
  • ✓Leave visible space, move on, and return—do not freeze
  • ✓Reserve the final minutes for totals, question numbers and annexures
