Arithmetic first

Mathematics notebook

RRB maths rewards clean setup and mental arithmetic. Search a topic, expand it, then solve the worked example without looking.

Trigonometry essentials
Core identities:sin²θ + cos²θ = 1  ·  1 + tan²θ = sec²θ  ·  1 + cot²θ = cosec²θStandard values: sin 30° = 1/2, sin 45° = 1/√2, sin 60° = √3/2; cos mirrors sin; tan 45° = 1.
Worked: A 10 m ladder makes 60° with the ground. Height reached = 10 · sin 60° = 5√3 ≈ 8.66 m.
Pipes & cisterns
Treat filling as work. Part filled in 1 hour = 1 ÷ hours to fill A pipe that empties subtracts: net rate = 1/a − 1/b.
Worked: Pipe A fills in 6 h, pipe B empties in 8 h. Net per hour = 1/6 − 1/8 = 1/24; the tank fills in 24 hours.
Age calculations
Set up with the present as base year. If A is x now, A was x − k "k years ago" and will be x + k "k years hence"
Worked: A father is 3 times his son's age; after 12 years he will be twice as old. 3x + 12 = 2(x + 12) → x = 12. Son is 12, father is 36.
Percentages & profit/loss
Percentage change:% change = (change ÷ original) × 100Successive changes: net % = a + b + ab/100, using negative values for decreases.Profit% = Profit ÷ CP × 100 · Discount% = Discount ÷ MP × 100
Worked: Price rises 20% then falls 20%. Net = 20 − 20 − 4 = −4%; ₹500 becomes ₹480.
Ratio, proportion & mixture
If a:b = m:n, write a = mk and b = nk. In direct proportion, x/y is constant; in inverse proportion, xy is constant.Alligation ratio = (dearer − mean) : (mean − cheaper)
Worked: Mix ₹40/kg and ₹70/kg rice to get ₹50/kg. Ratio = (70−50):(50−40) = 20:10 = 2:1.
Averages
Average = sum of observations ÷ number of observationsWhen one value changes, total changes by the same amount. Weighted average uses Σ(wx)/Σw.
Worked: Average of 8 numbers is 24. Replacing 18 with 30 adds 12 to total, so new average = 24 + 12/8 = 25.5.
Simple & compound interest
SI = PRT/100 · Amount(CI) = P(1 + R/100)ᵀFor 2 years, CI − SI = P(R/100)².
Worked: ₹5,000 at 10% compound interest for 2 years: 5000×1.1² = ₹6,050; CI = ₹1,050.
Time & work
If A finishes in x days, A’s one-day work is 1/x. Add rates when working together; subtract for leaks.Together time for A(x days), B(y days) = xy/(x+y)
Worked: A takes 12 days, B 18. Together = 216/30 = 7.2 days.
Speed, time & trains
Speed = distance/time · 1 m/s = 18/5 km/hTrain crossing a pole uses train length; crossing a platform uses train + platform length. Opposite direction: add speeds; same direction: subtract.
Worked: A 180 m train at 54 km/h crosses a pole in 180÷15 = 12 seconds.
Number system, HCF & LCM
For two positive integers: product = HCF × LCM. Divisibility: 3/9 use digit sum; 11 uses alternating digit sum.Euclid: gcd(a,b) = gcd(b, a mod b)
Worked: HCF of 84 and 126: 126 mod 84 = 42; 84 mod 42 = 0, so HCF = 42.
Algebra essentials
(a+b)²=a²+2ab+b² · (a−b)²=a²−2ab+b² · a²−b²=(a−b)(a+b)For ax+b=c, isolate x by inverse operations on both sides.
Worked: If x + 1/x = 5, then x² + 1/x² = 25 − 2 = 23.
Mensuration & geometry
Triangle area=½bh · Circle=πr² · Cylinder volume=πr²h · Cuboid=lbhSimilar figures: lengths scale k, areas k², volumes k³.
Worked: Radius doubles; circle area becomes 2² = 4 times.
Data interpretation
Read units and base totals first. Convert pie angle to value with angle/360 × total. Median is the middle ordered value; mode is most frequent.
Worked: A 72° sector from total 450 represents 72/360×450 = 90.
Patterns with proof

Reasoning toolkit

Write the rule beside every answer. A pattern you cannot state is only a guess.

Venn diagrams
Translate "all / some / no" into containment or overlap before reading the options. n(A ∪ B) = n(A) + n(B) − n(A ∩ B)
Worked: 60 students play cricket, 40 play football, 20 play both. Only cricket = 60 − 20 = 40.
Statements & assumptions
An assumption is unstated but necessary. Test it: would the statement collapse if the assumption were false?
Worked: Statement: "Join our course — 90% of users got a job." Assumption: the course caused the jobs. If users were already employable, the claim collapses.
Jumbled sentences (para jumbles)
Find the independent opener (no pronouns or demonstratives), then link pronoun → noun and cause → effect.
Worked: P: "He arrived late." Q: "Ravi had missed the bus." The name "Ravi" must precede "he": Q → P.
Number & alphabet series
Check first differences, second differences, multiplication, alternating terms and squares/cubes—in that order. Map A=1 … Z=26.
Worked: 3, 8, 15, 24, 35 → differences 5,7,9,11; next +13 = 48.
Coding–decoding
Test letter shift, reverse alphabet (A↔Z), position operations and word reordering. Apply the same transformation to every character.
Worked: If CAT → DBU (+1 each), TRAIN → USBJO.
Analogy & classification
State the relation precisely: object–function, part–whole, worker–tool, degree, cause–effect, or numeric operation.
Worked: Odometer : distance :: thermometer : temperature.
Syllogisms
Draw minimal Venn diagrams. “All A are B” does not mean all B are A. “Some” establishes existence; universal statements alone do not always do so.
Worked: All trains are vehicles; some vehicles are electric. “Some trains are electric” does not follow.
Direction sense
Use coordinates: north +y, east +x. Keep orientation separate from displacement.
Worked: 6 km east then 8 km north gives straight distance √(36+64)=10 km, north-east.
Blood relations
Build a tiny family tree using generations. Mark gender only when stated; never infer from a role-neutral word.
Worked: “My mother’s only son” is the speaker if male; otherwise the speaker’s brother. Context matters.
Ranking & order
Total people = rank from top + rank from bottom − 1
Worked: Riya is 12th from top and 19th from bottom: total = 12+19−1 = 30.
Clock & calendar
Clock angle = |30H − 5.5M|; choose the smaller angleOrdinary year shifts weekday by 1; leap year by 2 (when the interval includes Feb 29).
Worked: At 3:20, angle = |90−110| = 20°.
Statement & conclusion
Use only the given statement. A conclusion must necessarily follow, not merely sound reasonable. Watch absolute words: always, never, only.
Worked: “Some stations are crowded” cannot prove “all stations are crowded.”
Seating & puzzles
Create fixed slots, place absolute clues first, then relational clues. In circular seating facing centre, a person’s left is clockwise; facing outward reverses it.
Worked: If A is immediately left of B and both face centre, place A one clockwise seat from B.
Class 10 core

General science essentials

Group D science usually tests school-level Physics, Chemistry and Life Science. Learn definitions with units, causes and one application.

Physics

Motion & force

Speed is scalar; velocity has direction. Acceleration is change in velocity per unit time. Newton’s second law: F = ma. Momentum p = mv; impulse equals change in momentum.

Work = force × displacement · Power = work/time · KE = ½mv²
Physics

Electricity

Current is charge flow per second. Potential difference supplies energy per unit charge. In series, resistance adds; in parallel, reciprocals add.

V = IR · Power = VI = I²R = V²/R · Energy = Pt
Physics

Light & sound

Reflection obeys angle i = angle r. Convex lenses converge; concave lenses diverge. Sound needs a medium; pitch depends on frequency, loudness on amplitude.

Wave speed = frequency × wavelength
Chemistry

Atoms, elements & compounds

Atomic number = protons. Mass number = protons + neutrons. Isotopes share atomic number but differ in mass. Ionic bonds transfer electrons; covalent bonds share them.

Chemistry

Acids, bases & salts

Acids give H⁺ in water; bases give OH⁻. pH below 7 is acidic, 7 neutral, above 7 basic. Acid + base → salt + water.

pH = −log₁₀[H⁺]
Chemistry

Metals & reactions

Oxidation is loss of electrons; reduction is gain. More reactive metals displace less reactive metals from salts. Rusting needs oxygen and moisture.

Biology

Cell & tissues

Cell membrane controls exchange; nucleus holds genetic material; mitochondria release usable energy; chloroplasts perform photosynthesis. Tissues are groups of similar cells with a function.

Biology

Human systems

Alveoli exchange gases. Small intestine absorbs nutrients. Nephrons filter blood. Arteries carry blood away from the heart; veins return it. Insulin lowers blood glucose.

Biology

Heredity & ecology

Genes are units of heredity on chromosomes. Dominant traits appear in heterozygotes. Energy flows one way through food chains; only a fraction passes to the next trophic level.

Memory drill: write SI unit → formula → common instrument for each quantity: force–newton–spring balance; current–ampere–ammeter; potential difference–volt–voltmeter; power–watt–wattmeter; temperature–kelvin–thermometer.
Static GK + current events

General awareness foundation

Stable facts are built in below. For current affairs, revise the 6–8 months before your exam from reliable dated notes and the official notification.

ConstitutionAdopted 26 Nov 1949; in force 26 Jan 1950. Fundamental Rights: Part III. Directive Principles: Part IV.
ParliamentPresident + Lok Sabha + Rajya Sabha. Money Bills originate only in Lok Sabha; Speaker certifies them.
JudiciarySupreme Court is the apex court. Judicial review tests laws against the Constitution.
Freedom movementINC: 1885. Non-Cooperation: 1920. Civil Disobedience began with Dandi March: 1930. Quit India: 1942.
GeographyTropic of Cancer crosses 8 Indian states. Standard Meridian: 82°30′E. Indian Standard Time = UTC+5:30.
RiversHimalayan rivers are largely perennial; peninsular rivers are mainly rain-fed. Ganga–Brahmaputra forms a vast delta.
EconomyGDP measures final goods and services produced within a country. Inflation is a sustained rise in the general price level.
RBIMonetary policy influences liquidity and interest rates. Repo rate is the rate at which RBI lends short-term funds to banks against securities.
Computer basicsCPU executes instructions; RAM is volatile working memory; ROM is non-volatile. 8 bits = 1 byte.
EnvironmentProducers form the first trophic level. Ozone absorbs much harmful UV radiation. CO₂, methane and nitrous oxide are greenhouse gases.
Space & scienceEarth rotates west to east. A geostationary satellite orbits above the equator with the same period as Earth’s rotation.
Sports & awardsStudy winners, venues, trophies and governing bodies from the months before the exam—always attach a date to current facts.

Domain knowledge

Railway awareness capsule

High-value historical and operating-system basics. For organisation counts, zones and current office-holders, use the latest official material because these can change.

History timeline

1853

India’s first passenger train ran from Bombay (Bori Bunder) to Thane on 16 April.

1925

India’s first electric passenger train ran between Bombay VT and Kurla.

1951

Major railway systems were grouped into zones as the network was reorganised.

1986

Computerised passenger reservation began in New Delhi.

Track & operations vocabulary

Gauge: distance between inner faces of rails; broad gauge in India is 1,676 mm.

Block section: track portion between two block stations where train movement is controlled.

Points & crossings: track components that guide vehicles from one track to another.

Interlocking: arrangement preventing conflicting routes or unsafe signal operation.

Loco pilot: operates the locomotive/train; guard or train manager oversees train operations from the rear/end position according to duty.

Signals

Red: stop. Yellow: caution, be prepared for a restrictive indication. Green: proceed subject to rules. Never reduce real operating rules to colour alone.

Traction

Electric traction draws power through overhead equipment and pantograph. Diesel-electric locomotives use a diesel engine to generate electricity for traction motors.

Safety

Train protection combines signalling, interlocking, communication, braking discipline and human procedures. Kavach is India’s indigenous automatic train protection system.

Exam caution

Do not memorise stale “largest / longest / newest” lists

Those answers change with commissioning and reorganisation. Build your static foundation here, then verify any superlative or current railway fact from the current CEN or official railway publication close to your exam date.

Honest limitation: this hub covers the common aptitude base (maths, reasoning, general science, awareness, railway GK). Technical/trade syllabi for ALP, JE and technician posts — and mutable facts like zone counts or office-holders — must come from the current CEN and official material.

Study Plan

42-day route

Six weeks from concepts to mocks

Designed for 2½–3 hours daily. If you have less time, preserve the order and halve the question counts—not the review.

Week 1 · Baseline

  • Diagnostic test
  • Percentages, ratio, averages
  • Series, analogy, coding
  • Motion, force, cells
  • 300 static GK facts

Week 2 · Arithmetic

  • Profit/loss, SI/CI
  • Time & work
  • Speed, distance, trains
  • Directions, ranking
  • 2 sectional tests

Week 3 · Core science

  • Electricity, light, sound
  • Acids, bases, metals
  • Human systems
  • Syllogism, statements
  • 1 mixed mock

Week 4 · Coverage

  • Algebra, geometry
  • Data interpretation
  • History, polity, geography
  • Railway awareness
  • 2 mixed mocks

Week 5 · Speed

  • 30 maths questions/day
  • 30 reasoning questions/day
  • Science error log
  • Current-affairs revision
  • 3 full mocks

Week 6 · Exam mode

  • Alternate full mocks
  • Deep error analysis
  • Formula recall sheets
  • No new sources
  • Sleep at exam timing
