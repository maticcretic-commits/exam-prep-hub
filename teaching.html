Qualifying marks

General category candidates need 60% (90 of 150). Reserved categories get relaxation as per the official information bulletin.

When is it held?

CTET is usually held twice a year. Session dates change every cycle — confirm the current session on the official information bulletin before planning.

Certificate validity

The CTET eligibility certificate is valid for lifetime. There is no limit on the number of attempts.

Note: Clearing CTET makes you eligible to apply for teaching posts — it is not itself a job appointment. Recruitment happens through separate processes (e.g., BPSC TRE).

Study Material

The study library

Open a chapter, learn the idea, then rehearse the exam trap. Search by topic—try “Piaget”, “fractions” or “Bihar”.

01

Child development foundations

Stages · principles · theorists+

Development is patterned, not identical

Development is continuous, follows broad sequences, moves from general to specific responses and is shaped by heredity and environment. Children vary in pace; difference is not deficiency.

Three theorists in one glance

  • Piaget: sensorimotor (0–2), pre-operational (2–7), concrete operational (7–11), formal operational (11+). A primary child learns best through concrete material before symbols.
  • Vygotsky: learning is social. The ZPD is what a learner can do with support; scaffolding is gradually withdrawn.
  • Kohlberg: moral reasoning progresses from consequence/self-interest to social approval/law, then principled reasoning.
Exam lens: choose the option that respects the child’s reasoning and uses errors diagnostically—not punishment, labeling or one-method-for-all teaching.
02

Learning, assessment & inclusion

Constructivism · CWSN · evaluation+

Constructivist classroom

Learners connect new ideas to prior knowledge. The teacher designs inquiry, discussion, peer work and reflection rather than merely transmitting answers.

  • Formative assessment: during learning; feedback changes the next teaching step.
  • Summative assessment: after a unit/term; judges achievement.
  • Diagnostic assessment: identifies the source of a difficulty; remediation follows.
  • Portfolio: evidence of progress over time.

Inclusive practice

Keep the learning goal common while varying access: audio/text choice, larger print, tactile material, extra processing time, peer support and multiple ways to respond. Accommodation changes how a learner accesses the task; modification changes what is expected.

Remember: intelligence is multi-dimensional and developable. A test score is evidence from one setting, not a fixed label.
03

Language pedagogy

Acquisition · literacy · multilingualism+

Language grows through meaningful use

Listening and speaking support reading and writing. Children acquire patterns by using language in context; grammar is best taught through purposeful text, not isolated rule memorisation alone.

  • Before reading: activate prior knowledge, predict from title/pictures.
  • During: question, infer, notice connections, monitor meaning.
  • After: summarise, respond, compare viewpoints.
  • Multilingual class: use home languages as bridges; code-switching can scaffold meaning.
Skimming = gist · Scanning = specific detail · Inference = meaning not directly stated

Error treatment: first understand whether the error comes from transfer, overgeneralisation or incomplete rule knowledge; respond without humiliating the learner.

04

Mathematics & its pedagogy

Concepts · formulas · misconceptions+

Teach from concrete to abstract

Use the CPA sequence: Concrete objects → Pictorial models → Abstract symbols. Ask learners to explain strategies; multiple valid methods reveal mathematical thinking.

  • Fraction comparison: use a common denominator or compare distance from 0/1—not the size of denominator alone.
  • Place value: the value of digit 7 in 7,305 is 7,000; its face value remains 7.
  • Error analysis: “1/3 + 1/4 = 2/7” shows adding denominators; use area strips to rebuild the idea.
Percentage = (part ÷ whole) × 100
Average = sum ÷ number of values
Simple interest = P × R × T ÷ 100
Work rate: 1/Together = 1/A + 1/B
Rectangle area = l×b · Circle area = πr²
05

Environmental Studies

Themes · inquiry · daily life+

EVS integrates science and social life

Its recurring themes—Family & Friends, Food, Shelter, Water, Travel, and Things We Make and Do—begin with the child’s surroundings and expand outward.

  • Water: evaporation → condensation → precipitation → collection. Conservation works best at source: fix leaks, recharge groundwater, reuse suitable greywater.
  • Food chains: producers capture solar energy; consumers transfer it; decomposers recycle nutrients.
  • Field observation: observe, record, classify, compare, question, then explain. A worksheet alone is not inquiry.
  • Social understanding: avoid stereotypes about work, family roles, gender, caste, language or region.
Best answer pattern: local experience + hands-on investigation + discussion + reflection.
06

Science for Paper II

Core science · experiments · reasoning+

High-yield concept spine

  • Matter: particle spacing and motion explain solid, liquid and gas; heating increases average kinetic energy.
  • Force & motion: net force changes velocity. Friction can hinder motion but also enables walking and gripping.
  • Life: cells are structural/functional units; photosynthesis stores light energy; respiration releases usable energy.
  • Electricity: current requires a closed circuit; series components share current, parallel branches share voltage.

Science pedagogy

A fair test changes one variable, controls relevant others and measures the outcome. Let learners predict before observing; a surprising result is an opportunity to revise a model.

Speed = distance ÷ time · Density = mass ÷ volume · V = I×R
07

Social Science for Paper II

History · geography · civics+

Build arguments from evidence

  • History: sources include inscriptions, coins, texts, objects and oral traditions. A source reflects a context and point of view.
  • Geography: latitude measures north–south position; longitude helps determine local time. Monsoon is a seasonal wind reversal, not simply “heavy rain”.
  • Civics: the Constitution limits state power and protects rights; federalism divides authority across levels.
  • Economics: distinguish need/want, paid/unpaid work, formal/informal sectors and public/private provision.

Pedagogy: use timelines for change, maps for spatial relationships, role-play for institutions and contrasting sources for critical thinking.

08

BPSC TRE: GS & Bihar core

Bihar facts · arithmetic · reasoning+

Bihar anchor points

  • Bihar was separated from Bengal on 22 March 1912; Bihar Day is observed on 22 March.
  • The Champaran Satyagraha (1917) challenged indigo cultivation arrangements and marked Gandhi’s first satyagraha in India.
  • Nalanda and Vikramashila were major centres of learning in ancient/early medieval eastern India.
  • The Ganga flows west to east through the state; the Kosi is noted for shifting channels and flood impact.

GS solving order

Start with direct facts, then arithmetic, then multi-step reasoning. In percentage change, always identify the original value as the base.

Successive change a%, b% = a + b + ab/100
Population density = population ÷ area
Distance = speed × time

TRE language qualifier

English · Hindi · comprehension+

English quick rules

  • Subject–verb agreement follows the true head noun: “The quality of the books is good.”
  • Passive voice: object → subject + suitable form of be + past participle.
  • Reported speech shifts tense when the reporting verb is past, unless the statement remains universally true.
  • Read the question before the passage; answer from the passage, not outside knowledge.

हिंदी त्वरित पुनरावृत्ति

  • संधि: दो वर्णों के मेल से होने वाला परिवर्तन।
  • समास: दो या अधिक पदों का संक्षिप्त रूप; विग्रह से संबंध स्पष्ट करें।
  • उपसर्ग शब्द के पहले, प्रत्यय शब्द के बाद जुड़ता है।
  • गद्यांश में उत्तर पाठ के संकेतों से चुनें; केवल सामान्य ज्ञान पर निर्भर न रहें।

Study Plan

A four-week revision route

Designed for a working aspirant: two 45-minute blocks on weekdays, one mock-and-review block on the weekend.

WEEK 01

Build the base

  • Development principles + Piaget/Vygotsky
  • Language acquisition and comprehension
  • Number sense / Bihar anchor facts
  • Weekend: 50-question baseline
WEEK 02

Teach each subject

  • Math misconceptions and manipulatives
  • EVS inquiry and themes
  • Science or Social Science pedagogy
  • Weekend: review every wrong answer
WEEK 03

Mix and retrieve

  • Alternate content and pedagogy sets
  • Language accuracy drills
  • TRE GS arithmetic and reasoning
  • Weekend: timed full paper
WEEK 04

Close the gaps

  • Revise error notebook, not everything
  • Two timed section tests
  • Memorise formulas and distinctions
  • Last day: light recall and rest

Pedagogy: the high-scoring distinctions

  • Growth is mainly quantitative; development includes qualitative change.
  • Readiness is not waiting passively; teaching and support can extend capability.
  • Equality gives the same resource; equity gives support needed for fair access.
  • Assessment of learning judges; for learning guides; as learning builds self-monitoring.
  • Intrinsic motivation comes from interest/mastery; extrinsic motivation comes from external reward or consequence.
  • Divergent questions allow several reasoned answers; convergent questions narrow to one.

Think like the examiner

When two answers look plausible, prefer the one that:

Observesfinds the learner’s current idea
Scaffoldssupports without doing the task
Reflectsuses evidence to plan next steps

Distractors often use absolutes—“always”, “only”, “all children”—or confuse silence and memorisation with learning.
