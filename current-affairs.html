    Chapter 1: Indian Polity & Governance, 2025–26

    Polity questions in 2025–26 exams revolve around three pivots: the landmark Bihar Assembly election of November 2025, the institutional reforms announced in the Union Budget 2026–27 and the 16th Finance Commission award, and the steady implementation of the new criminal codes and labour codes. Memorise dates, seat tallies and constitutional articles — these are direct MCQ fodder.

    1.1 Bihar Assembly Election 2025: the headline verdict

    Bihar went to the polls in two phases on 6 and 11 November 2025, with counting on 14 November 2025. The BJP-led National Democratic Alliance (NDA) won 202 of the 243 seats, defeating the RJD-led Mahagathbandhan, which managed only 35. The majority mark is 122, so the NDA's tally was close to a three-fourths majority — its best showing since the 2010 sweep of 206 seats.

Nitish Kumar was sworn in as Chief Minister for a record tenth time on about 20 November 2025, with Samrat Choudhary and Vijay Kumar Sinha as Deputy CMs. Then, in a twist reported about five months later (roughly April 2026), Nitish resigned after being elected to the Rajya Sabha and Samrat Choudhary became Bihar's first BJP Chief Minister, with Vijay Kumar Chaudhary and Bijendra Prasad Yadav as deputies (as reported; confirm the final ministry composition from the latest gazette before citing it in an answer).

    1.2 The 16th Finance Commission and Centre–State finances

    The 16th Finance Commission (Chairman: Arvind Panagariya, constituted December 2023) submitted its report covering the award period 2026–27 to 2030–31, and Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman tabled it in the Lok Sabha alongside the Union Budget 2026–27. The report governs the vertical and horizontal devolution of net tax proceeds to the states for the next five years — a standard exam question on every finance commission's award period.

    1.3 New criminal laws and labour codes in force

    The three new criminal laws — the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS, replacing IPC 1860), the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS, replacing CrPC 1973) and the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA, replacing the Indian Evidence Act 1872) — came into force on 1 July 2024, and 2025–26 papers test their practical implementation: zero-FIR filing anywhere in India, e-FIRs, community service as a new punishment under BNS, and videography of search and seizure made mandatory. Separately, the government consolidated 29 labour laws into 4 labour codes (Wages; Industrial Relations; Social Security; OSH), notified for implementation from 2025 — the Budget 2026–27 speech referred to the "reform express" including GST reforms and labour codes as firmly on track.

    1.4 Census 2027 and other institutional notes

    The Union Cabinet approved caste enumeration as part of the next Census — the first caste census since 1931 — with Census 2027 to be conducted in two phases (as announced in 2025). India celebrated its 77th Republic Day on 26 January 2026. Also remember: the Women's Reservation Act (Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam, 2023) reserves one-third of seats in the Lok Sabha and state assemblies, to take effect after the next delimitation exercise following the census — a classic "which statement is correct" trap in polity sections.

    1.5 Electoral institutions and the Vice-Presidential election

    Gyanesh Kumar took charge as Chief Election Commissioner in February 2025 (as reported), succeeding Rajiv Kumar. The ECI's Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls, begun in Bihar in mid-2025 ahead of the Assembly polls, became a major political and legal flashpoint — remember the acronym SIR and the state where it started. In September 2025, C. P. Radhakrishnan was elected Vice-President of India (as reported), succeeding Jagdeep Dhankhar who resigned in July 2025 citing health reasons — the first mid-term Vice-Presidential election since 1987. The Vice-President is elected by an electoral college of both Houses of Parliament (MPs only, unlike the President's wider college) under Article 66 — a standard static-current bridge.

    Chapter 2: Indian Economy & Union Budget 2026–27

    The economy chapter is the highest-yield section of any current-affairs paper. The centrepiece is Union Budget 2026–27, presented by Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman on 1 February 2026 — her record ninth consecutive Budget — built around three "kartavyas": accelerating economic growth, fulfilling people's aspirations, and inclusive development under "Sabka Saath, Sabka Vikas". The Economic Survey projected real GDP growth of 6.8–7.2% for 2026–27.

    2.2 Tax and trade reforms

    The biggest structural announcement was the new Income Tax Act, 2025, to take effect from 1 April 2026, replacing the 1961 Act with a simplified regime. On customs: the tariff structure was simplified, personal import duty was cut from 20% to 10%, duty-free import limits for seafood export inputs were raised to 3% of FOB turnover, customs exemptions were extended for lithium-ion battery storage, solar glass and nuclear power projects until 2035, and customs duty was waived on 17 drugs with seven more rare diseases added for duty-free personal imports. Interest awarded by Motor Accident Claims Tribunals was made exempt from income tax.

    2.4 Infrastructure and rail

    The Budget announced seven new high-speed rail corridors plus a dedicated freight corridor, alongside "rare earth corridors" — a direct response to global critical-mineral supply anxieties. Memorise the pairing: 7 high-speed corridors + 1 freight corridor. For context, India's first high-speed line remains the Mumbai–Ahmedabad bullet train corridor, targeted for phased opening.

    2.5 Macro backdrop

    As reported through 2025, India remained the world's fastest-growing major economy and was widely reported to have become the fourth-largest economy (overtaking Japan) — phrase it as "as reported by NITI Aayog/official projections" since agencies differ on timing. The RBI's monetary stance through the period stayed focused on the 4% CPI inflation target (±2% band); candidates should track the current repo rate from the latest MPC statement rather than memorising a stale figure. Note the Budget's explicit framing: the "reform express" of GST, labour codes and quality control orders since 15 August 2025.

    2.6 External sector, RBI and digital economy

    Through 2025–26 India's foreign-exchange reserves hovered around USD 690–700 billion (as reported), among the world's largest, and the rupee remained under pressure from the strong dollar and tariff uncertainty — track the RBI's reference rate from its releases rather than memorising a level. UPI continued its record run, processing over 16 billion transactions a month by late 2025 (as reported), with NPCI expanding UPI acceptance to more countries (UAE, Singapore, France and others in the linkage pipeline). Monthly GST collections stayed above ₹1.8 lakh crore through much of the period (as reported), reflecting the "GST 2.0" rate rationalisation the Budget speech alluded to. On trade, the India–UK CETA (signed 24 July 2025) and the reported conclusion of the India–EU FTA negotiations framed the export-diversification story against US tariff headwinds.

    Chapter 3: Science & Technology, 2025–26

    Science questions in 2025–26 cluster around four stories: India's human spaceflight milestones, the IndiaAI Mission and the AI Impact Summit, the semiconductor and quantum missions, and defence R&D. Names, mission designations and "firsts" are the scoring points.

    3.1 Space: Axiom-4 and Gaganyaan

    Group Captain Shubhanshu Shukla became the first Indian to visit the International Space Station as pilot of Axiom Mission 4 (launched June 2025), and only the second Indian in space after Rakesh Sharma (1984). He was subsequently conferred the Ashoka Chakra (announced in the 2026 gallantry list) — the first astronaut to receive India's highest peacetime gallantry award. ISRO's own crewed programme, Gaganyaan, advanced through its uncrewed test-flight sequence (TV-D1 crew-escape demonstration was 2023; the G-1 uncrewed orbital mission has been the programme's pacing item — cite its status only from the latest ISRO update). Also track: Chandrayaan-4 (lunar sample-return mission, approved by the Cabinet in 2024) and the Bharatiya Antariksh Station module plan for the 2030s.

    3.2 AI: the India AI Impact Summit 2026

    The India AI Impact Summit was held at Bharat Mandapam, New Delhi, from 16–21 February 2026 — the first global AI summit hosted in the Global South, pivoting the agenda from Western "AI safety" to AI for development. Key memorables: delegations from 100+ countries and 20 international organisations; the New Delhi Declaration endorsed by about 92 countries and organisations (including the USA, UK, China and the EU — a diplomatic first, since some had declined the 2025 Paris statement); over USD 200 billion in AI investment pledges (Reliance ~USD 110 billion over seven years, Adani ~USD 100 billion by 2035, Google's USD 15 billion AI hub in Visakhapatnam plus new India–US subsea cable routes); India joined the US-led Pax Silica coalition for critical-mineral and semiconductor supply chains; and 13 frontier-model developers signed the New Delhi Frontier AI Impact Commitments. Sam Altman noted India is OpenAI's fastest-growing Codex market. The summit also set a Guinness record for AI responsibility pledges. India's domestic pillar is the IndiaAI Mission (approved 2024, ~₹10,372 crore), building sovereign compute and the BharatGen multilingual model family.

    3.3 Semiconductors, quantum and deep tech

    Budget 2026–27 launched India Semiconductor Mission 2.0 (equipment and materials, building on ISM 1.0's fab/OSAT incentives) and raised the Electronics Components Manufacturing Scheme to ₹40,000 crore. The National Quantum Mission (approved 2023, ~₹6,003 crore) targets 50–1000 qubit quantum computers and quantum communication over 2,000 km. Memorise the pairing ISM 2.0 = equipment & materials; Budget 2026 = rare-earth corridors — both answer the same "critical minerals" question from different angles.

    3.4 Defence and health R&D

    2025–26 saw accelerated indigenous defence testing under the Atmanirbhar Bharat push (DRDO's Agni-series, Akash, and the Tejas Mk-1A induction programme — cite specific test dates from PIB only). In health-tech, the Biopharma SHAKTI scheme (₹10,000 crore) aims at biologics and biosimilars manufacturing, and the Budget added 3 new NIPERs. Note: customs duty exemptions for nuclear power projects were extended to 2035, signalling the small-modular-reactor (SMR) push for the 100 GW nuclear target by 2047.

    3.5 Space docking, DPI and deep-tech institutions

    In January 2025 ISRO achieved the SpaDeX docking — India became the fourth country to demonstrate space docking (after the USA, Russia and China), a prerequisite for the Bharatiya Antariksh Station and Chandrayaan-4's lunar sample-return profile (as reported). On digital public infrastructure, ONDC crossed expanding order volumes in e-commerce and mobility, and Account Aggregator plus OCEN frameworks deepened credit access — papers increasingly ask what each DPI layer does: identity (Aadhaar), payments (UPI), data sharing (AA/DEPA), commerce (ONDC). The National Research Foundation (NRF, now operational as ANRF) began funding high-risk research, and the Deep Tech Fund of Funds announced in Budget 2025–26 targeted startups in semiconductors, AI and space. Remember the institutional ladder: DST → ANRF → sectoral missions (NQM, ISM, IndiaAI).

    Chapter 4: Environment & Climate, 2025–26

    Environment questions test conventions, COP outcomes, flagship species programmes and disaster events. The anchor events of this cycle are COP30 at Belém (Brazil), the collapse of the global plastics treaty talks, and India's record monsoon and heat extremes.

    4.1 COP30, Belém (November 2025)

    The 30th UN Climate Conference (COP30) was held in Belém, Brazil, in November 2025 — the first COP in the Amazon. Its framing was the Global Stocktake follow-through from COP28's "transition away from fossil fuels" language and the new climate finance goal (NCQG) agreed at COP29 in Baku (USD 300 billion/year by 2035, widely criticised by developing countries as inadequate). For exams, memorise the sequence: COP28 Dubai (2023) — fossil-fuel transition; COP29 Baku (2024) — NCQG finance; COP30 Belém (2025) — Amazon, adaptation and implementation review. India's consistent negotiating line: equity, common but differentiated responsibilities (CBDR), and the LiFE (Lifestyle for Environment) mission launched at COP26. As with all COP outcomes, quote final decisions from UNFCCC documents, not press summaries.

    4.2 Plastics treaty: the Geneva failure

    The resumed fifth session of the Intergovernmental Negotiating Committee (INC-5.2, Geneva, August 2025) ended without a legally binding global plastics treaty, the second collapse after Busan (INC-5, 2024). The deadlock: production caps versus waste-management-only approaches. This is a favourite "which statement is correct" topic — remember that as of late 2026 there is no binding global plastics treaty.

    4.3 India: monsoon, heat and forests

    India's 2025 southwest monsoon was reported above normal (IMD's long-period-average based assessment), bringing both bumper kharif prospects and severe flooding in several states — the classic "monsoon and agriculture" linkage question. The country also recorded extreme heat episodes in 2024–25, with the IMD expanding its heat-action-plan advisories. On the conservation side, memorise India's standing tallies from official releases: Project Tiger reserves (58, with the newest additions announced 2024–25) — verify the current count from the NTCA before the exam — and India's Ramsar sites tally, which crossed 90 sites (India has the largest Ramsar network in Asia). The International Big Cat Alliance (IBCA), headquartered in India and launched in 2023, entered its operational phase covering seven big cats: tiger, lion, leopard, snow leopard, cheetah, jaguar and puma.

    4.4 Budget and policy signals

    Budget 2026–27 allocated ₹20,000 crore for carbon capture and utilisation and extended customs exemptions for nuclear power projects to 2035 — both are climate-policy markers: nuclear as firm low-carbon power and CCU for hard-to-abate industry. The Mission LiFE and Green Hydrogen Mission (2030 target: 5 million metric tonnes/year) remain India's flagship domestic climate programmes. Also note the 2025 amendment-track of environmental clearance and EIA processes — a recurring governance-environment crossover question.

    4.5 Disasters, air quality and urban environment

    The 2025 monsoon cycle brought severe flooding in Punjab, Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand and Jammu & Kashmir (as reported), with cloudburst-led flash floods recurring as a Himalayan pattern — link this to the static topic of western disturbances and orographic rainfall. Delhi-NCR's winter again triggered GRAP (Graded Response Action Plan) stages as AQI crossed "severe" thresholds; GRAP is implemented by the Commission for Air Quality Management (CAQM), a statutory body under the 2021 Act — a favourite agency-based question. The National Clean Air Programme (NCAP) target (40% PM reduction in 131 cities by 2025–26 versus 2017 levels) entered its assessment phase. Also track the 2025 amendments to the Forest (Conservation) framework and the EIA notification process — environment-governance crossover questions increasingly cite these.

    4.6 Clean energy and green missions

    India's energy transition supplied several exam facts: non-fossil installed capacity crossed 50% of total power capacity in 2025 (as reported), five years ahead of the 2030 NDC target — link this to the Panchamrit pledges from COP26 (500 GW non-fossil by 2030; 50% electric-energy from renewables; 1 billion tonnes emission reduction; 45% emission-intensity cut; net zero by 2070). The National Green Hydrogen Mission (2030: 5 MMT/year production, ₹19,744 crore) advanced with pilot refuelling and SIGHT auction tranches. The Budget 2026–27 added ₹20,000 crore for carbon capture and utilisation and nuclear customs relief to 2035. Also remember the Ecomark rules and the Green Credit Programme — both ask "which ministry" questions (Environment, Forest & Climate Change).

    11.1 The monthly-compilation method (how toppers study current affairs)

    3. Monthly categories: file every fact under fixed heads — Polity, Economy, S&T, Environment, Sports, Awards, Indices, State (Bihar), IR, Schemes. Fixed heads make retrieval automatic in the exam hall.

    5 Oct 2026 — Karl Deisseroth (54, American), Peter Hegemann (71, German) and Georg Nagel (73, German) won the 2026 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine “for discoveries concerning light-gated ion channels and optogenetics”, announced by the Nobel Assembly at Sweden's Karolinska Institute.     In 2025 India overtook Japan to become the world's fourth-largest economy, behind the US, China and Germany, as announced by NITI Aayog citing IMF data. With FY25 GDP growth of 6.5%, India remains the fastest-growing major economy and eyes the third spot by the decade's end.

    economy

    RBI's 2025 rate cuts

    New Governor Sanjay Malhotra's RBI cut the repo rate by a cumulative 125 bps in 2025 — 25 bps in February, 25 bps in April, 50 bps in June and 25 bps in December — bringing it from 6.50% to 5.25% as inflation cooled. The RBI also paid a record Rs 2.69 lakh crore surplus to the government for FY25.

    economy

    GST 2.0 slabs

    In September 2025 the GST Council approved a simplified two-slab structure — 5% and 18% — effective 22 September 2025, scrapping the 12% and 28% slabs. A 40% slab covers sin and luxury goods. The overhaul, billed as GST 2.0, cut taxes on daily essentials, cementing a pre-festive consumption boost.

    economy

    India-UK free trade deal

    India and the UK concluded a Comprehensive Economic and Trade Agreement, signed in July 2025 during PM Modi's London visit. It slashes duties on Indian textiles, leather and engineering goods and opens UK whisky, cars and services. It is India's biggest trade deal with a developed economy in years.

    economy

    US tariffs on India 2025

    In August 2025 the US imposed 50% tariffs on Indian goods — 25% reciprocal plus 25% penalty over Russian oil imports — among the highest rates on any trade partner. The move hit textiles, gems and jewellery, and engineering exports, and became the central irritant in India-US ties.

    Budget 2025 tax relief

    Budget 2025-26 made annual income up to Rs 12 lakh tax-free under the new regime (Rs 12.75 lakh for salaried taxpayers with the standard deduction). The Budget also set the FY26 fiscal deficit target at 4.4% of GDP and announced the 100-GW nuclear mission and Gyan Bharatam.

UPI is live in 7+ countries (UAE, Singapore, Sri Lanka, Mauritius, Nepal, France, Bhutan) and RBI aims to take it to 20 countries by 2028-29.

    Exam strategy

    How to turn this library into marks — the repeatable method behind every topper's current-affairs routine.

    1. Build the monthly spine

    Read chapters 11–12 for the Jan–Sep 2026 month-by-month flow first. Then deepen one subject chapter per day — polity and economy before everything else, since they carry the most questions.

    2. Test before you revise

    Attempt 40–50 MCQs untimed, then read every explanation — including for questions you got right. The explanations are where the real syllabus lives.

    3. Three-pass revision

    Pass 1: capsules by category. Pass 2: one-liners end to end. Pass 3: re-attempt only the questions you missed. Keep each pass under a week so facts stay fresh.

    4. Source-first habit

    For any disputed fact, check PIB or the ministry site before a coaching PDF. The verify badges (⚠) in the question bank mark exactly the facts worth this treatment.

    5. Map to your exam

    BPSC/State PCS aspirants: weight the Bihar-special chapter and capsules hardest. SSC/Bank: weight awards, sports, indices and one-liners. UPSC: weight polity, economy and environment chapters with mains framing.

    6. The 10-minute daily loop

    One chapter section + 20 one-liners + 10 MCQs every day, forever. Current affairs rewards the last 60 days most — never let the chain break in the final two months.
