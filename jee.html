Overview & Dates

JEE Main · Paper 1

2026 reference pattern · always check the next NTA bulletin

  • Computer-based, 3 hours, Physics/Chemistry/Mathematics.
  • Each subject: 20 MCQs + 5 numerical-value questions.
  • 75 questions, 300 marks; +4 correct, −1 incorrect, 0 unattempted.
  • Use it for NIT/IIIT/CFTI admission and as the qualifying route to JEE Advanced.

  • Both papers cover Physics, Chemistry and Mathematics.
  • Expect single-correct, multi-correct and numerical-answer formats.
  • Read each section’s marking rules before attempting; partial/negative marks may differ.

Study Material

Physics

Draw the system, choose axes, state the law, then calculate. Units and limiting cases are your fastest error checks.

Kinematics describes motion; dynamics explains it. For constant acceleration, eliminate time when possible. In circular motion, “centripetal force” is not a new force—it is the inward net component of real forces.

Work–energy is usually shortest when forces vary with position. Momentum is strongest for short interactions and isolated systems.

v = u + at · v² = u² + 2as · Wnet = ΔK
p = mv · J = Δp · τ = Iα · L = Iω

Advanced move: separate centre-of-mass translation from rotation; use constraints before writing too many equations.

SHM requires acceleration proportional and opposite to displacement. Energy swaps between kinetic and potential while total mechanical energy stays fixed. Travelling waves transport energy, not material.

a = −ω²x · T = 2π√(m/k) · vwave = fλ
P + ½ρv² + ρgh = constant

Advanced move: derive effective spring constants and use phase, not only amplitude, when superposing oscillations.

The first law is energy accounting. Fix the sign convention before substitution. For ideal gases, internal energy depends only on temperature; the path controls heat and work.

PV = nRT · ΔQ = ΔU + Wby
Cp − Cv = R · ηCarnot = 1 − Tc/Th

Trap: isothermal does not mean zero heat; adiabatic does not mean constant temperature.

Fields add as vectors; potentials add as scalars. Use symmetry with Gauss’s law only when the field’s magnitude is constant on the chosen surface. In circuits, reduce only genuine series/parallel groups.

F = kq₁q₂/r² · E = −dV/dr · C = Q/V
V = IR · P = VI = I²R · Ucap = ½CV²

Advanced move: for capacitor networks, track charge conservation on isolated conductor nodes.

Magnetic force changes direction but does no work on a point charge. Faraday–Lenz gives induced emf and its opposition to changing flux. In AC, phasors encode phase difference.

F = q(v × B) · Fwire = I(L × B)
ε = −dΦ/dt · XL = ωL · XC = 1/(ωC)

Resonance: series RLC has XL = XC, minimum impedance and maximum current.

Geometric optics follows rays and sign conventions; wave optics follows phase and path difference. Modern physics is compact and scoring when formulas are tied to physical meaning.

1/f = 1/v − 1/u · β = λD/d
Kmax = hν − φ · λ = h/p · E = Δmc²

Advanced move: test image nature before applying magnification signs; in interference, count phase reversals at reflection.

Semiconductors in one minute. Intrinsic: pure Si/Ge. n-type: pentavalent dopant, electrons in the majority; p-type: trivalent dopant, holes in the majority. A p–n junction conducts in forward bias and blocks in reverse. Zener diode: voltage regulation in reverse breakdown. Logic: AND is 1 only if all inputs are 1; OR is 1 if any input is 1; NOT inverts; NAND and NOR are universal gates.

Chemistry

Physical is model + calculation, organic is mechanism + pattern, inorganic is structure + disciplined recall.

Convert every quantity to moles, use the balanced equation, identify the limiting reagent, then convert back. Equivalent concepts are shortcuts—not substitutes for balancing.

n = mass/M = N/NA · Molarity = moles/L solution
% yield = actual/theoretical × 100

Advanced move: use atom balance and charge balance together for mixed redox systems.

Quantum numbers identify allowed states. Periodic trends arise from effective nuclear charge, shielding and shell size. Bond shape follows electron-domain repulsion; bond order follows orbital occupancy.

Eₙ(H-like) = −13.6Z²/n² eV
Bond order = ½(Nbonding − Nantibonding)

Trap: geometry, hybridisation and magnetic behaviour must agree with electron count.

Spontaneity at fixed temperature and pressure follows Gibbs energy. Equilibrium constants depend on temperature, not initial concentration. Electrochemical potential measures redox driving force.

ΔG = ΔH − TΔS · ΔG° = −RT lnK
Ecell = E°cell − (RT/nF)lnQ · pH = −log[H⁺]

Advanced move: build an ICE table only after deciding which approximation is physically justified.

Rate law is experimental; stoichiometry alone cannot supply orders. Colligative properties depend on particle count. The van’t Hoff factor diagnoses association or dissociation.

rate = k[A]^m[B]^n · t½(first order) = 0.693/k
ΔTb = iKb m · π = iCRT

Trap: units of k change with overall order.

Start with electron density: nucleophile donates, electrophile accepts. Compare carbocation stability, leaving-group ability, steric access and solvent before naming a mechanism.

SN1: rate ∝ [substrate] · SN2: rate ∝ [substrate][Nu]
Markovnikov: H adds to carbon already richer in H (ionic addition)

Advanced move: track isotope, stereochemistry and rearrangement; they distinguish mechanisms.

Biomolecules & polymers. Glucose is an aldohexose; fructose a ketohexose. Proteins are α-amino-acid polymers joined by peptide bonds. DNA pairs A–T, G–C; RNA pairs A–U, G–C. Addition polymers: polythene, PVC, teflon. Condensation polymers: nylon-6,6, terylene, bakelite.

Organise facts by periodic trend, oxidation state, colour/magnetism and reaction type. For coordination compounds: oxidation number → d count → ligand field → spin → magnetic moment.

μspin-only = √[n(n+2)] BM
EAN = Z − oxidation state + donated ligand electrons

Method: keep a one-page exception log; reread NCERT wording for p-block and qualitative chemistry.

d- and f-block quick facts. CrO₄²⁻ is yellow and Cr₂O₇²⁻ orange (Cr is +6 in both). KMnO₄ is purple and oxidises in acid; K₂Cr₂O₇ is a strong oxidant in acid. Zn, Cd and Hg (d¹⁰) are not typical transition metals. Lanthanoid contraction makes 4d and 5d radii nearly equal — the reason 5d chemistry mirrors 4d chemistry.

Mathematics

Translate the condition, choose a representation, restrict the domain, then solve. A sketch often saves a page.

Complex numbers turn algebra into geometry on the Argand plane. For sequences, search for a telescoping difference or known mean. Matrices encode linear transformations; determinants encode scaling and invertibility.

z = r(cosθ + i sinθ) · zⁿ = rⁿ(cos nθ + i sin nθ)
ΣAP = n/2[2a+(n−1)d] · A⁻¹ = adj(A)/|A|

Advanced move: use root symmetry and Vieta before expanding.

Domain is part of the answer. A limit asks local behaviour, continuity joins value to limit, and differentiability asks for a unique local slope. Derivatives optimise after critical points and boundaries are checked.

lim(x→0) sinx/x = 1 · d(uv)=u dv+v du
dy/dx = (dy/dt)/(dx/dt) · tangent: y−y₁=m(x−x₁)

Trap: squaring can add roots; logarithms and inverse trig impose domains.

Integration reverses differentiation and accumulates area. Before choosing substitution or parts, simplify algebra and inspect derivative patterns. Definite-integral symmetry is a major time saver.

∫u dv = uv − ∫v du · ∫₀ᵃ f(x)dx = ∫₀ᵃ f(a−x)dx
dy/dx + Py = Q ⇒ IF = e^(∫Pdx)

Advanced move: pair I with its transformed form under x→a−x or x→1/x.

Translate geometry into distances, slopes and parameters. Standard conics are focus–directrix loci; parametric points keep algebra controlled.

circle: (x−h)²+(y−k)²=r² · parabola: y²=4ax
ellipse: x²/a²+y²/b²=1 · hyperbola: x²/a²−y²/b²=1

Advanced move: use tangent parameters rather than substituting a general line and expanding.

Dot product measures projection; cross product measures oriented area; scalar triple product measures volume. In 3D lines, test coplanarity before seeking intersection.

a·b = |a||b|cosθ · |a×b|=|a||b|sinθ
plane: r·n=d · volume = |a·(b×c)|

Trap: shortest distance requires the common perpendicular direction.

First define equally likely outcomes or conditional structure. Count arrangements with restrictions by complementary counting or slot methods. Expectation is linear even when variables are dependent.

P(A|B)=P(A∩B)/P(B) · P(A∪B)=P(A)+P(B)−P(A∩B)
ⁿCᵣ=n!/[r!(n−r)!] · E(X)=ΣxP(X=x)

Advanced move: condition on the first decisive event to build recurrences.

Statistics basics. Mean = Σx/n. Variance = Σ(x − mean)²/n (population) — the standard deviation is its square root. For a frequency table, weight each value by its frequency. Median: the middle value when ordered; mode: the most frequent value.

Practice

1. A particle starts from rest with constant acceleration 4 m/s². Its speed after travelling 18 m is:

12 m/s. Use v²=u²+2as = 0+2×4×18 = 144.

2. For a first-order reaction, doubling the initial concentration changes the half-life by:

No change. For first order, t½=0.693/k, independent of initial concentration.

3. If z = 1 + i, then |z⁴| equals:

4. |z|=√2, so |z⁴|=|z|⁴=(√2)⁴=4.

4. A capacitor C charged to V is disconnected, then plate separation is doubled. Its stored energy becomes:

2U. Disconnected means Q stays fixed. Doubling separation halves C, and U=Q²/(2C) doubles.

5. Which species has the highest bond order?

O₂⁺. Removing one electron from an antibonding π* orbital raises bond order from 2 to 2.5.

6. The maximum value of sin x + cos x is:

√2. Write sin x + cos x = √2 sin(x+π/4).

7. A solid sphere rolls without slipping. The fraction of its total kinetic energy that is rotational is:

2/7. I = 2/5 mR² and v = ωR, so K_rot = ½Iω² = 1/5 mv² while K_total = 1/2 mv² + 1/5 mv² = 7/10 mv².

8. In SHM of amplitude A, the displacement where kinetic energy equals potential energy is:

A/√2. Set ½k(A² − x²) = ½kx², giving x² = A²/2.

9. For N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃ at equilibrium, increasing the pressure shifts the equilibrium toward:

The products. Higher pressure favours the side with fewer gas moles — 2 mol on the product side against 4 on the reactant side.

10. The oxidation state of chromium in K₂Cr₂O₇ is:

+6. 2(+1) + 2x + 7(−2) = 0 gives x = +6.

11. The value of ∫₀π/2 sin x dx is:

1. [−cos x]₀π/2 = −cos(π/2) + cos(0) = 0 + 1.

12. If |a| = 3, |b| = 4 and a·b = 6, the angle between a and b is:

60°. cos θ = (a·b)/(|a||b|) = 6/12 = 0.5.

LIVE SCORE0/12

Answer all twelve. A wrong answer is useful if you can name the reason.

Twelve questions, no notes. Choose once; explanations appear immediately. The point is not the score — it is identifying whether your next hour belongs to concepts, algebra or careful reading.

Study Plan

WEEKS 1–8

Foundation

  1. Learn one concept block.
  2. Do 20–30 basic-to-Main problems.
  3. Write a five-line recall sheet.
  4. Retest errors after 48 hours.
WEEKS 9–16

Integration

  1. Mix two chapters per session.
  2. Add numerical and multi-correct sets.
  3. Run one 90-minute subject test weekly.
  4. Tag errors: concept, algebra, reading, time.
WEEKS 17–24

Exam mode

  1. Alternate Main and Advanced papers.
  2. Simulate exact breaks and timings.
  3. Revise from error log, not from scratch.
  4. Stop low-value resource switching.
SAMPLE DAY
Block A · 90 min
New concept + worked examples
Block B · 75 min
Timed problem set
Block C · 45 min
Error log + spaced recall
